This provides an abstraction layer between the set-theoretic filtering equations and their numerical realization, so… ▽ More We introduce Contractor Filters (CFs), a set-membership architecture for nonlinear continuous-time state estimation with bounded uncertainties and sporadic outliers. CFs represent feasible states symbolically by compositions of contractors rather than rematerializing a geometric enclosure after each update. This provides an abstraction layer between the set-theoretic filtering equations and their numerical realization, so prediction, correction, ambiguity, and outlier handling can be expressed within one algebra without prescribing a state geometry. We derive an exact nominal recursion and a guaranteed robust enclosure, with validated flow inclusions preserving soundness. Optional sub-paving compression then provides finite outer representations. CFs are evaluated against a robust particle filter on range-only and terrain-based localization under outliers, probabilistic model mismatch, association ambiguity, and multimodality. CFs retain the simulated truth in all tested configurations, while particle-filter reliability degrades under persistent bias and spatial aliasing. Contractor strength and compression budget control the trade-off between paving tightness and computational cost, while preserving the enclosure guarantee. △ Less

    Entrapment and Dissolution of Microbubbles Inside Microwells

In this work, we investigate the entrapment of microbubbles on a hydrophobic surface, structured with microwells, when water flow passes along, and the… ▽ More The formation and evolution of immersed surface micro- and nanobubbles are essential in various practical applications, such as the usage of superhydrophobic rematerials, drug delivery, and mineral flotation. In this work, we investigate the entrapment of microbubbles on a hydrophobic surface, structured with microwells, when water flow passes along, and the subsequent microbubble dissolution. At entrapment, the microbubble is initially pinned at the edge of the microwell. At some point, the three-phase contact line detaches from one side of the edge and separates from the wall, after which it further recedes. We systematically investigate the evolution of the footprint diameter and the contact angle of the entrapped microbubbles, which reveals that the dissolution process is in the constant contact angle mode. By varying the gas undersaturation level, we quantify how a high gas undersaturation enhances the dissolution process, and compare with simplified theoretical predictions for dissolving bubbles on a plane surface. We find that geometric partial blockage effects of the diffusive flux out of the microbubble trapped in the microwell lead to reduced dissolution rates. △ Less
