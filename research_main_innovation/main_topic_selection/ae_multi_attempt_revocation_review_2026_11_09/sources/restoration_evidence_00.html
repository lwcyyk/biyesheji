Restoration Evidence Records for AI Agent Incident Response

Abstract

When an AI agent system is stopped, restricted or has its credentials revoked during an incident, someone later decides whether it may run again. Incident response guidance asks for that decision to be documented with the risk that remains, but no format says what the record must contain for a party outside the deploying organization to check it. This document defines five record types for that purpose: an incident action authorization, a revocation sweep, a re-enable decision, a joint authority assignment for incidents that span organizations, and a revocation boundary report. Each is an in-toto Statement carried in a DSSE envelope, uses the canonical form of the agent audit record, and is checked by ordered rules with a named reason for each failure. A bundle rule set binds a re-enable decision to the records it cites, by digest. A published conformance corpus carries a reject member for every rule that a member can force.¶

1. Introduction

The agent audit record [AUDIT-RECORD] records one authorization decision and its observed effect. An incident adds decisions that record does not carry: who authorized a pause or a revocation, which credentials the revocation reached, who decided that the stopped system may resume and on what evidence, and, when two organizations are involved, which of them may act on which asset. These decisions are made over time, by different parties, and they refer to one another. The proposed charter of the AUDIT working group [AUDIT-CHARTER] names "authorization state over time" among the things its data models should represent; this document covers that state for the period from containment to restoration. Section 11 of [AIMS] likewise requires durable audit logs covering "authorization decisions and subsequent remediations" and leaves their form open; the records here give the remediation half a form.¶

Incident response guidance asks for these decisions and leaves their form open. NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 [NIST-SP-800-61R3] has handlers work with system owners to confirm restoration (RC.RP-04) and declares the end of recovery "based on criteria" (RC.RP-06). NIST SP 800-184 [NIST-SP-800-184] describes restored assets being validated "before receiving approval to restore network operations" (Section 6.2.2) without naming the approver. The NIST, NSA and CISA Key Practices for incidents in agent training and evaluation [KEY-PRACTICES] ask that containment layers be shown working before agents restart (7.3), that new controls be shown to catch a replay of the incident (7.4), and that the recovery decision be documented with the remaining risks (7.5). None of these texts says what that documentation must contain for a party that trusts neither the agent nor its operator to check it.¶

This document fixes that record. It defines five predicate types and the rules a reader applies to each, and a set of rules for a bundle of records that together support one re-enable decision. It specifies no admission policy: whether a given decision is enough to let a system run again is the relying party's own policy, written against these records.¶

The goals are:¶

  • to name, in each decision, the parties who held each role and the kind of grant each authorizer acted on, so that "the team decided" becomes a statement someone can be held to;¶

  • to make a revocation's reach visible from the record alone, so that a claim wider than the evidence is refused;¶

  • to make an omitted restart condition distinguishable from one the decision-maker chose not to address;¶

  • to bind a restart decision to the authorization and revocation records it rests on, by digest, so the chain can be checked offline.¶

1.1. Relationship to the agent audit record

This document uses the canonical form, statement shape and envelope of [AUDIT-RECORD] and does not amend it. The predicate type of that document is frozen (Section 8 of [AUDIT-RECORD]), so the record types defined here are separate predicate types and do not extend it. A re-enable decision (R3) MAY cite an agent audit record as evidence by its digest (Section 5.3), which is how an enforced action and the decision that authorized resuming it are joined.¶

2. Conventions and Definitions

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.¶

3. Terminology

Containment:

An action that limits what an agent system can do while an incident is handled: a pause, a restriction of a credential, permission or tool, or an isolation of channels.¶

Restoration:

The decision to end a containment wholly or in part. It is separate from containment and from remediation and does not follow from either.¶

Producer:

The party that signs a record.¶

Reader:

A party that checks a record against this document.¶

Relying party:

A party that decides what to do on the basis of a checked record, under its own admission policy.¶

Bundle:

One re-enable decision record together with every record it names as a predecessor, recursively, and any joint authority or revocation boundary record the producer adds.¶

Closed value:

A value drawn from a set this document or [VOCABULARY] enumerates. A value outside the set makes the record malformed.¶

4. Common Structure

4.1. Statement and envelope

A record is an in-toto Statement v1 [IN-TOTO] carried in a DSSE envelope [DSSE]. The statement MUST have exactly the members _type, subject, predicateType and predicate. _type MUST be https://in-toto.io/Statement/v1, and predicateType MUST be one of the five URIs of Section 5.¶

Statements are I-JSON [RFC7493] and are canonicalized as in Section 4.1 of [AUDIT-RECORD], using JCS [RFC8785]. A duplicate member at any depth makes the statement malformed. Times are RFC 3339 [RFC3339] UTC times to whole seconds and are compared as instants.¶

A member this document does not define for a type MUST be refused. This departs from the in-toto parsing rule that consumers ignore unknown fields: a revision that adds a member publishes it under a new predicate type URI (Section 9), so a reader of a v1 type never meets a member it has not been told how to read.¶

Each subject member carries a digest holding only sha256. What the digest is taken over is stated for each type.¶

4.2. Common predicate members

Every predicate defined here carries these members:¶

incident:

An object with issuer, an https URL naming who assigns the identifier; id, a non-empty string; and, optionally, related, a list of further {issuer, id} pairs naming the same incident in another organization's records. This is the term incident_identifier of [VOCABULARY].¶

producer:

An object with id, an https URL naming the signing party.¶

issuedAt:

The time the record was issued.¶

registryDigest:

An object with sha256, the SHA-256 of the exact bytes of the [VOCABULARY] registry the record's closed values are expressed in.¶

doesNotAssert:

A list of non-empty strings, each stating something the producer says the record does not claim. The list MAY be empty; the member MUST NOT be absent.¶

Where a type uses it, predecessors is a list of {predicateType, digest} naming earlier records of this document, where digest holds the sha256 of the predecessor statement's bytes exactly as carried in its DSSE payload.¶

4.3. Closed values

The values of response_function, pause_hold, restoration_decision, revocation_state, intent_binding, independence_basis and set_sufficiency are those of the registry whose digest is 1c5ff80bd97237a603afc402db692ffe5abe15e4a7f9eca13f20068ada347fdc, published under https://w3id.org/agent-evidence/vocab/v1/registry/ with the file name sha256-, then the digest, then .yaml. Values that a section of this document lists itself are closed by this document.¶

A reader MUST refuse a value outside a closed set and MUST NOT ignore the member. Registration in [VOCABULARY] gives a value its identity; which values a member accepts is set by this document alone.¶

4.4. Rule order and verdicts

Each type has an ordered rule table. A reader applies the rules in order and reports the first that fails, with its reason code. Rules 1 to 3 are the same for every type:¶

Table 1
# Rule Code
1 The statement has the shape of Section 4.1 and the predicate type of this section statement-malformed
2 The predicate has exactly its members, each well formed predicate-malformed
3 Every closed value is in its set closed-vocabulary-violation

A record that passes every rule of its type is accepted with the verdict named for the type. Two readers that apply this document reach the same verdict and the same first failing reason on the same bytes; the conformance corpus (Section 10) tests that.¶

5.1. R1: Incident action authorization

An R1 records who authorized one incident response action, which role each named party held, on what kind of grant the authorizer acted, and whether the authority was committed before the action ran.¶

The subject is the operation authorized: one or more members, each the SHA-256 of the operation's declared description.¶

The predicate carries the common members and:¶

action:

functions, a non-empty sorted list of response_function values (monitoring_increase, restriction, isolation, pause, remediation, restoration); targets, a non-empty list of {id} naming the agents, tools, routes or credentials acted on; and inducedEffects, which is one of {"declared": "listed", "items": [...]}, {"declared": "none_observed", "observation": {"sha256": ...}} or {"declared": "unknown"}. A credential revocation is recorded as restriction, and its reach as an R2.¶

parties:

A non-empty list of {id, roles, authorityBasis?, independenceBasis?}. roles is a non-empty sorted list drawn from advised, authorized, executed and accountable. One party MAY hold several roles. authorityBasis is one of role_grant, policy_preauthorized, contract_third_party, executive_approval and unknown, and MUST be present on every party holding authorized. independenceBasis is an independence_basis value.¶

authorizedAt, notAfter:

When the authority was given, and when it lapses.¶

executedAt:

Optional. When the action ran.¶

intentBinding:

An intent_binding value: committed_before_execution or asserted_after_execution.¶

pause:

Optional. {hold, holdProof}, where hold is a pause_hold value and holdProof is {sha256} of the observation that established it.¶

Table 2
# Rule Code
4 At least one party holds authorized no-authorizer
5 At least one party holds accountable no-accountable-party
6 Every party holding authorized carries authorityBasis authority-basis-missing
7 authorizedAt is not after notAfter authorization-window-inverted
8 When intentBinding is committed_before_execution and executedAt is present, executedAt is not before authorizedAt intent-not-committed-before
9 When executedAt is present, it is not after notAfter authorization-expired
10 inducedEffects declared none_observed carries observation induced-effects-unobserved

A record passing every rule is accepted as authorization-recorded. A reader MUST NOT read an absent authorityBasis as role_grant; the value for not knowing is unknown.¶

5.2. R2: Revocation sweep

An R2 records which credentials of a declared population were revoked during an incident, which were not, and how far the producer claims the sweep reaches.¶

The subject is the population manifest: one member, the SHA-256 of the list of credential identifiers the sweep was run over. Identifiers are opaque names; they MUST NOT be secrets, hashes of secrets or key fingerprints.¶

The predicate carries the common members, predecessors (at least one R1, the authorization of the sweep), and:¶

population:

credentials, a non-empty, sorted, duplicate-free list of identifiers.¶

partition:

A list of {state, credentials} parts, state one of revoked, retained and unassessed.¶

revocations:

One entry per revoked credential: credential; revokedAt; evidence, a resource descriptor {digest, uri?, mediaType?} of the deny record or policy change; and relyingParties, a list of {id, state} with state a revocation_state value (issued, enforced_observed, use_after_revocation, not_established).¶

coverage:

claim, one of complete_verified, issued_after_only, partial and unknown; and issuedAfter, a time, present when claim is issued_after_only.¶

Table 3
# Rule Code
4 The parts are disjoint and their union equals population.credentials denominator-union-mismatch
5 revocations names exactly the credentials of the revoked part revocation-entries-mismatch
6 complete_verified is claimed only when every credential is in the revoked part coverage-claim-exceeds-proof
7 issued_after_only carries issuedAfter coverage-claim-incomplete

A record passing every rule is accepted as sweep-recorded. An honest partial or issued_after_only sweep is well formed. Whether a relying party admits a system restored under it is that party's policy.¶

5.3. R3: Re-enable decision

An R3 records one decision about whether a stopped or restricted agent system may run again: the outcome, the scope restored, who approved it and on what independence, and, for every kind of evidence incident guidance asks a restart to show, either the digest of what was shown or an explicit statement that it was not addressed.¶

The subject is the configuration restored: one or more members, each the SHA-256 of a baseline (model weights, harness code, container image, tool configuration).¶

The predicate carries the common members, predecessors (at least one record of this document), and:¶

restoration:

decision, a restoration_decision value (resume_full, resume_staged, remain_restricted, not_established); and restoredScope, present when decision is resume_staged: {agents, tools, routes, credentials}, each a sorted list of identifiers, at least one non-empty.¶

approvers:

A list of {id, roles, independenceBasis}, with roles as in R1 and independenceBasis an independence_basis value (independent_sources, subject_supplied, undeclared).¶

setSufficiency:

The producer's own set_sufficiency judgment of the evidence below: sufficient, insufficient or not_assessed.¶

evidence:

An object with exactly the sixteen slots of the table below. Each slot is either a resource descriptor, {"digest": {"sha256": ...}} with optional uri and mediaType as in the in-toto ResourceDescriptor, or {"notAddressed": "<reason>"} with a non-empty reason. A slot MAY point at an attestation the producer already emits, including an agent audit record [AUDIT-RECORD].¶

A record passing every rule is accepted as decision-recorded. The slots record what a decision cites. Which slots a relying party requires to carry a digest, and what it does with a notAddressed slot, is its own admission policy. A record that omits a slot is malformed; a record that declares a slot not addressed says so in a form the relying party can refuse.¶

5.4. R4: Joint authority

An R4 records, for an incident that crosses organizations, which party may act on which asset, under what kind of grant, and which indicators the parties share. Restart conditions in incident guidance assume one owner of the restored asset; when an agent operated by one organization affects infrastructure run by another, an R4 names the owners and the assignment, so each party's R1 and R3 records can be read against it.¶

The subject is the shared indicator set: one member, the SHA-256 of the canonical list of shared indicators.¶

The predicate carries the common members and:¶

sharedIndicators:

A non-empty sorted list of {sha256} digests of indicators both parties hold.¶

parties:

At least two entries {id, organization, correlationId, authorityBasis, assets}, authorityBasis as in R1 and assets a sorted list of asset identifiers the party may act on.¶

actions:

Optional. A list of {party, asset, record} naming actions already taken, where record is {sha256} of the R1 statement that authorized the action.¶

Table 6
# Rule Code
4 Every party carries authorityBasis authority-basis-missing
5 The parties belong to at least two organizations single-organization
6 Every action names a party in parties and an asset in that party's assets party-acts-outside-assignment

A record passing every rule is accepted as assignment-recorded. A record co-signed by every party (several DSSE signatures over one payload) is stronger than one signed by one party, and the envelope shows which it is.¶

5.5. RB: Revocation boundary

Revoking a credential stops new work from starting under it. It does not by itself stop work already in flight from committing its effect afterwards. An RB reports, for one declared sink and one test window, whether any effect from an in-flight task committed at that sink's declared commit boundary after the revocation took effect. It states an outcome and requires no mechanism.¶

The subject is the test plan: one member, the SHA-256 of the declared sink and window.¶

The predicate carries the common members, optional predecessors, and:¶

sink:

{id, kind, commitBoundary}. kind is one of database, filesystem, http_endpoint, subprocess, queue and other. commitBoundary is a non-empty string saying at which point an effect counts as committed.¶

observer:

{id, vantage}, vantage one of sink_side and agent_side.¶

revocation:

credential, an opaque identifier; effectiveAt, when the revocation took effect at its authority; and timeSource, a non-empty string naming the clock.¶

window:

{start, end}.¶

counts:

Non-negative integers attempts, inFlightAtRevocation, postRevocationCommits, replaysAttempted and replaysCommitted.¶

exclusions:

A list of {count, reason}, each reason non-empty, for attempts left out of the counts.¶

result:

One of no_post_revocation_commit_observed, post_revocation_commit_observed and not_established.¶

Table 7
# Rule Code
4 window.start is not after effectiveAt, and effectiveAt is not after window.end revocation-outside-window
5 inFlightAtRevocation does not exceed attempts, postRevocationCommits does not exceed inFlightAtRevocation, and replaysCommitted does not exceed replaysAttempted counts-inconsistent
6 result is post_revocation_commit_observed exactly when postRevocationCommits or replaysCommitted is positive, unless result is not_established result-contradicts-counts
7 no_post_revocation_commit_observed requires a sink_side observer observer-not-at-sink

A record passing every rule is accepted as boundary-recorded. One record covers one sink; a claim about several sinks is several records.¶

6. Bundles

A re-enable decision is rarely checkable alone: it rests on the authorizations and revocations before it. A restoration evidence bundle is one R3 together with every record its predecessors name, recursively, and any R4 or RB the producer adds. A reader checks a bundle in this order and reports the first failure:¶

Table 8
# Rule Code
B1 Every record in the bundle conforms to its own type the record's own code
B2 Every predecessor digest resolves to exactly one record carried in the bundle predecessor-not-carried
B3 The record a digest resolves to has the predicateType the reference names predecessor-type-mismatch
B4 No record names itself, directly or through other records, as its predecessor predecessor-cycle
B5 Every record names the same incident, by incident or by one of its related pairs incident-mismatch
B6 No record's issuedAt is earlier than that of a record it names as a predecessor predecessor-issued-later
B7 Every credential an R3 restores in restoredScope.credentials appears in some R2 in the bundle restored-credential-unswept

A bundle passing every rule is reported as bundle-consistent. That verdict says the records agree with each other. It does not say whether the relying party should admit the decision.¶

A relying party's admission policy is written against a consistent bundle. For example, a policy can require that every restored credential lie in the revoked part of an R2 whose coverage.claim is complete_verified, that containmentReproof and incidentReplay carry digests, and that at least one approver with independent_sources hold authorized. Such a policy is outside this document.¶

7. Security Considerations

7.1. A signature authenticates an assertion, not its truth

Every record here is a statement by its producer. A conforming record is complete and internally consistent; it is not evidence that the authority named was held, that the action ran as described, that the cited evidence shows what its slot names, or that resuming was safe. The value of the format is that a false statement is attributable to a named signer and that an omission is visible, not that falsehood is prevented.¶

7.2. Absence is not read as a value

An absent authorityBasis is not role_grant, an omitted evidence slot is malformed rather than satisfied, and a claim of no induced effects requires the observation that looked. Each of these closes a way for a record to look complete because it says less. A reader that supplies a default for an absent member defeats the format.¶

7.3. Revocation reach

A revocation sweep that claims more than its partition shows is refused (R2 rule 6). A sweep that honestly reports issued_after_only or partial is well formed, and a relying party that admits a restart under it accepts that credentials issued earlier, or outside the revoked part, may still be live. The relyingParties states distinguish a revocation that was issued from one a relying party was observed to enforce; use_after_revocation exists so that a use after revocation can be recorded rather than omitted.¶

7.4. Work in flight

Revocation does not stop work that is already in flight from committing. An RB with a sink_side observer reports whether that happened at one sink in one window. A zero count bounds a rate for that sink and window; it is not a guarantee for other sinks or windows. An agent-side observer cannot support no_post_revocation_commit_observed (RB rule 7), because the agent process cannot see the sink's committed state.¶

7.5. Authority that outlives a pause

An R1 authorization carries notAfter. A relying party that admits actions under an R1 after a pause and restart SHOULD check notAfter against the restart, and SHOULD require a fresh R1 for any action after an R3, so that a permit issued before the pause is not reused after it.¶

7.6. Approver independence

independenceBasis is the producer's statement. A relying party that requires independence SHOULD require independent_sources and SHOULD resolve the named approver itself. An approver who also stopped the system and approves its restart is recordable; whether that is acceptable is admission policy.¶

7.7. Predecessor digests

Predecessors are named by the SHA-256 of the statement bytes as carried in the DSSE payload. A reader MUST hash those bytes, not a re-serialization, since a re-serialization that differs in member order or number form changes the digest.¶

8. Privacy Considerations

Records name parties by identifier and may name natural persons as authorizers, approvers or accountable parties. Producers SHOULD use role or account identifiers where a person's name is not needed by the relying party, and SHOULD state in doesNotAssert what a distributed record deliberately omits.¶

Credential identifiers in an R2 or RB are opaque names. They MUST NOT carry secrets, hashes of secrets or key fingerprints, because a record may travel to parties not entitled to the credential.¶

Evidence slots and indicator sets are bound by digest and not carried. A record can therefore travel to a party entitled to check the decision without carrying the forensic material the decision rests on; that party resolves a digest only if it needs the content. Shared indicators in an R4 are digests for the same reason: the record shows the parties hold the same indicators without disclosing them to a reader that does not.¶

9. IANA Considerations

This document has no IANA actions. Each predicate type is a URI under a namespace the author controls and requires no registration to be used, consistent with the in-toto Attestation Framework's treatment of predicate type identifiers [IN-TOTO].¶

A record carries its predicate type inside the bytes its producer signed. A later revision of this document MUST NOT move a URI once records carrying it exist, since moving it invalidates every signature over them. A change to what a record of a type must carry, including a member or a closed value added, is published under a new URI with v1 read as v2, and a record valid under a v1 URI stays valid under every later revision that keeps that URI.¶

10. Conformance

A conforming producer emits records that meet every MUST of Section 4 and of the section of each type it emits. A conforming reader applies the rule order of Section 4.4 to each record and the order of Section 6 to each bundle, and reports the first failing reason code.¶

A reader is not described as conforming to this revision unless it reaches the verdict and the reason its manifest declares on every member of the vectors-restart-records family of release 0.31.2 of [VECTORS]. That family carries 57 members, 15 to accept and 42 to reject, with corpus digest 5b7f58c91b517bd7c96dcccdef0d827a4f730fd4a650b4992b362bb9925cae29. Each reject member changes exactly one value of an accepting control, and the rule it names is the first that fails. Three rules have no member, and the manifest lists each with its reason: rule 3 of R4 and of RB, which carry no registry-defined value, and bundle rule B4, which would need a SHA-256 fixed point to force. Passing the corpus is necessary and not sufficient; those three rules are checked by reading them.¶

A document that references this one normatively SHOULD name the revision it relies on and MAY name a release of [VECTORS] as its conformance test. The five predicate type URIs, the reason codes, and the section anchors common, rule-order, r1, r2, r3, r4, rb and bundles are stable across revisions and may be referenced by name.¶

12. Mapping to the AUDIT Problem Statement

The AUDIT BoF request [AUDIT-BOFREQ] and the proposed charter [AUDIT-CHARTER] name problems a data model should address. This document addresses three of them for the period from containment to restoration:¶

  • Authorization state over time: R1 records each authorization with its window, R2 the revocation of credentials and its reach, R3 the decision that ends a containment, and the bundle rules bind them in order by digest and time.¶

  • References to the identities of participating entities: R1 and R3 name each party with its role and its authority basis, and R4 names the organizations and the assets each may act on.¶

  • Verification by a party that trusts neither the agent nor its operator: every record is signed, every closed value is fail-closed, and every rule has a reason code a second reader reproduces.¶

Action provenance for each enforced request is the subject of [AUDIT-RECORD]; an R3 cites such records by digest. Policy models, admission policy and retention periods are left to other work.¶
