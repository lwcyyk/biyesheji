Signed Permissions and Action Receipts for Automated Agents

Abstract

This document specifies records with which a third party can establish, later and without network access, what an automated agent, such as an artificial intelligence (AI) agent, was permitted to do and what it recorded doing. The person for whom the agent acts, the principal, signs a permission with a passkey; the permission carries limits that a verifier can check mechanically. The agent signs a receipt for each action with Ed25519 and the Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA-87). Receipts are chained and entered in a log whose signed tree heads are time-stamped. A verifier reports, separately, whether the records are sound and whether each recorded action stayed within its permission. The content of each record is specified independently of its encoding; one encoding, JSON Web Signature (JWS) over canonical JavaScript Object Notation (JSON), is defined.¶

1. Introduction

A principal signs a permission for an agent: which actions, with which services, within which limits, from when until when. The agent signs a receipt for each action it takes; a service may countersign it. A recorder appends receipts to a log and periodically signs its tree head, which a Time-Stamping Authority (TSA) time-stamps. Later, with no network access, a verifier checks the records and answers three separate questions: whether the records are sound, whether every signature could be verified on that device, and whether each recorded action stayed within its permission.¶

This document specifies the records and the verifier's rules: evidence, not enforcement. Nothing in this document stops an action when it is attempted, and an action for which no receipt was written leaves no trace in the agent's own log (Section 18). It names the role of an enforcement point that refuses an action before it takes effect, an executor (Section 2), but does not specify how an executor decides or keeps its records, how records are carried, how keys and trust anchors are distributed, or what to do about a finding (Section 15).¶

When an automated agent acts for a person, the questions asked afterward are the same: was the action authorized, by whom, within what limits, did the other party agree, and when? Authorization protocols answer at the moment of a request, typically with tokens that are not kept as evidence. This document specifies records that a third party can verify later, without network access. Each record is signed by the party that made it. Whoever keeps the log can still leave out or delay entries; Section 18 states how far that reaches.¶

The content model (Section 4 through Section 10) says what each record holds, who signs it, and how a verifier judges it, apart from how records are written as bytes. This document defines one encoding (Section 11): JSON Web Signature (JWS) [RFC7515] over JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) [RFC8259] in one canonical form [RFC8785]; Section 12 states what another encoding, such as one with Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR) Object Signing and Encryption (COSE), has to define. Logs use the Merkle tree of [RFC9162], and time-stamps come from a TSA [RFC3161]. Agents sign with Ed25519 [RFC8032] and the Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm ML-DSA-87 [FIPS204] side by side; a signed tree head adds the Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Algorithm SLH-DSA [FIPS205].¶

Other drafts (Section 13) already have principal-signed authorizations, signed and hash-chained receipts for each action, passkey approval of one exact action, signed budgets enforced when an action is attempted, a cumulative limit checked afterward against the amounts that a decision point recorded, and delegation that only narrows. This document adds three things. First, the permission that the principal signs with a passkey [WebAuthn] carries limits that a verifier can check mechanically (totals, single amounts, counts, and totals or counts within a rolling period) and conditions (the other party's countersignature, or the principal's approval of one action). Second, a cancellation can be acknowledged by the agent. Third, it specifies a verifier that adds up the signed receipts themselves and says, from the records alone and after the fact, whether each recorded action, including a helper agent's, stayed within the permission.¶

The records are evidence, not a verdict: a sound record can show an agent acting outside its permission. This document is the core of a published format description [FORMAT]; Section 15 lists the parts specified only there.¶

2. Conventions and Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.¶

The identifiers that [FORMAT] uses in labels and member names are given in parentheses after each term.¶

Principal:

The person who signs a permission with a passkey ("issuer").¶

Passkey:

A credential [WebAuthn] in a device, unlocked by user verification such as a biometric or a personal identification number (PIN).¶

Agent:

Software that acts for the principal. It deals with services, which may countersign its receipts.¶

Helper agent, or helper:

An agent that acts under a delegation (Section 7).¶

Service:

The other party to an action, named in a permission's "with"; it may countersign (Section 6).¶

Executor:

Software, other than the agent, that takes actions for the agent or holds what they need, such as a credential, and refuses an action that its check of the permission does not allow; [FORMAT] specifies one such check (Section 15). A permission names an executor in "with", as it names a service; to countersign, the executor needs its key set there (Section 6).¶

Permission:

The principal's signed statement of which agent may do what, within which limits, with whom, and when ("slip").¶

Action receipt, or receipt:

The agent's signed statement that it took one action ("stub"). It is not a Receipt in the sense of Supply Chain Integrity, Transparency, and Trust (SCITT) [RFC9943] (Section 13).¶

Delegation:

An agent's signed hand-over of part of its permission ("pass").¶

Record:

A signed statement of one of the eight kinds of Section 4.1.¶

Log:

A sequence of entries, only ever added to ("book").¶

Entry:

One record together with what is carried beside it, as one element of a log (Section 9.1).¶

Recorder:

Whoever keeps a log.¶

Signed tree head, or head:

The recorder's signed statement of a log's size and root ("seal").¶

Chain:

The receipts of one agent under one permission or one delegation, each naming the one before (Section 6).¶

Verifier:

Anyone, or any software, that verifies records ("checker").¶

Core verifier:

A verifier that implements this document and none of the parts specified only in [FORMAT] (Section 15).¶

Digest:

The SHA-256 hash [FIPS180-4] of some bytes.¶

Encoding:

How records, keys, and logs are written as bytes and how signatures are carried (Section 11, Section 12).¶

Problem:

A reason why an entry, or the log, cannot be relied on.¶

Sound:

Of an entry or a log: having no problem.¶

Finding:

A statement that a sound receipt or delegation lies outside its permission. A finding is not a problem.¶

Compared:

Of a receipt or delegation: judged against its permission (Section 10.1).¶

Counted:

Of a time-stamp: from a trusted TSA, on a record whose signatures were all verified (Section 9.3).¶

Intact:

Of a log: no problem was found and everything was verified (Section 10.1).¶

The base64url encoding is that of Section 5 of [RFC4648], without padding. Where a rule is followed by a code in parentheses, such as "(chain-broken)", an entry that breaks the rule has a problem. A verifier MUST report that code for it, unless it stopped at an earlier problem of the same entry (Section 10.1). Every finding of a compared receipt or delegation is reported: each code once, except over-period-limit, which is reported once for each period limit exceeded. Appendix A lists the codes.¶

3. Overview and Example

Once the principal has signed a permission, the agent signs a receipt for each action, naming the permission and the receipt before it. The recorder writes each receipt, with any countersignature and approval, as one log entry, and periodically appends a time-stamped signed tree head. This example follows the demonstration published with [FORMAT]; the parties and orders are invented. An office agent may order supplies up to 200 GBP in total and 100 GBP for one order, and send two messages in any hour. Every order needs the supplier's countersignature, and an order above 60 GBP the principal's approval. The permission's content is shown in the JSON encoding (Section 11), with spaces and line breaks added, and values abbreviated:¶

{"actions": ["supplies.order", "message.send"],
 "agent": {"keys": ["<Ed25519>", "<ML-DSA-87>"], "name": "Agent"},
 "id": "<base64url>",
 "issuer": {"key": "<ES256>", "name": "A. Person",
   "origin": "https://sign.example.org", "rpId": "example.org"},
 "limits": [
   {"action": "supplies.order", "max": 200, "unit": "GBP"},
   {"action": "supplies.order", "each": 100, "unit": "GBP"},
   {"action": "message.send", "count": 2, "per": 3600}],
 "never": ["destroys", "impersonate"],
 "purpose": "Keep the office stocked with paper and toner.",
 "requires": [
   {"action": "supplies.order", "need": "countersignature"},
   {"above": 60, "action": "supplies.order", "need": "approval",
    "unit": "GBP"}],
 "type": "provared.slip.v0",
 "validFrom": "2026-10-05T09:00:00Z",
 "validUntil": "2026-10-12T09:00:00Z",
 "with": [{"id": "supplier", "keys": ["<Ed25519>", "<ML-DSA-87>"],
   "name": "Example Stationery"}]}
¶

The log then holds these entries ("receipt n" has "seq" n; "cs" is a countersignature):¶

Entry Record     Action, amount With it      Total Findings
0     permission
1     receipt 0  order, 45 GBP  cs              45
2     receipt 1  message
3     receipt 2  order, 80 GBP  approval, cs   125
4     receipt 3  order, 25 GBP                 150 countersignature-
                                                   missing
5     receipt 4  order, 80 GBP  cs             230 over-limit,
                                                   approval-missing
6     signed tree head          time-stamp
¶

The content of the receipt at entry 5, shown across lines, is:¶

{"action":"supplies.order","amount":{"unit":"GBP","value":80},
 "id":"<base64url>","previous":"<digest of the receipt at 4>",
 "seq":4,"slip":"<digest of the permission>",
 "type":"provared.stub.v0","when":"2026-10-05T09:40:00Z",
 "with":"supplier"}
¶

The countersignature at entry 5 has the content {"stub": "<digest of the receipt at 5>", "type": "provared.countersignature.v0", "when": "2026-10-05T09:40:12Z"}.¶

Given the thumbprint of the principal's passkey, the digest of the recorder's key set, and the digest of the TSA's certificate (Section 4.4), a verifier finds no problem and verifies every signature: the log is intact. It reports that the agent did not stay within its permission, at entries 4 and 5.¶

4. Common Rules

Section 4 through Section 10 are the content model; Section 11 is the JSON encoding. Maps are written here as in JSON: {"unit", "value"} is a map with exactly those two members.¶

4.1. Records and Signers

A record is of one of eight kinds. Each is signed by one party, with these methods, in this order; X is the identifier of the kind in the labels of [FORMAT]:¶

Table 1: Kinds of record and their signers
Kind of record X Signed with
Permission slip the principal's passkey (Section 4.7)
Action receipt stub Ed25519, ML-DSA-87
Countersignature countersignature Ed25519, ML-DSA-87
Approval approval the principal's passkey
Delegation pass Ed25519, ML-DSA-87
Cancellation cancellation the principal's passkey
Acknowledgement acknowledgement Ed25519, ML-DSA-87
Signed tree head seal Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s

The content of a record holds a member "type" whose value is "provared.X.v0". Every signature of a record covers its kind as well as its content, so that a signature made for one kind of record is not accepted as a signature on another; the encoding says how (Section 11.1). The suffix "v0" marks this draft, so that a record of a later, frozen version is distinguished from a draft one.¶

4.2. Content

The content of a record is a map from member names, which are text, to values. A value is a map of the same kind, a list of values, text, an integer from 0 to 2^53 - 1, or one of the values of Section 4.3. There are no others: no true, false, or null, no fractions, and no negative numbers. Text is a sequence of Unicode scalar values; its length is counted in code points. The encoding writes the content in one form, which a verifier tests.¶

"type" MUST match the kind of record that the encoding labels (payload-type-mismatch). A map MUST hold exactly the members listed for it, each in its stated form (bad-field).¶

4.3. Values

Bytes:

A sequence of bytes, of the length stated for each member. Identifiers, digests, and the parts of keys are bytes.¶

Identifier:

An "id" is 16 random bytes. No two records in a log hold the same "id" (duplicate-id).¶

Time:

A date and time in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), to the second, with no fraction and no offset. It MUST denote a moment that exists: 29 February in a year that is not a leap year, an hour of 24, and a second of 60 are refused (bad-field).¶

Digests:

A digest is 32 bytes. A record's digest is that of its content, in the bytes that the encoding writes and that its signatures cover; it covers no signature. Records name one another by it. A "sha256" member holds a document's digest: records never hold documents.¶

Name:

Text of 1 to 200 code points, chosen by the writer. Except for the reserved names of documents (Section 6), a name is not checked against anything.¶

Amount:

Exactly {"unit", "value"}, where "value" is an integer and the unit is 1 to 16 ASCII letters, digits, ".", "-", or "_", beginning with a letter or digit.¶

4.4. Keys

A key is a public key of one of these methods, and of no other (bad-key):¶

Ed25519:

32 bytes ([RFC8032]).¶

ML-DSA-87:

2,592 bytes ([FIPS204]).¶

SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s:

64 bytes ([FIPS205]).¶

ES256:

A P-256 public key, two coordinates of 32 bytes (Section 3.4 of [RFC7518]).¶

RS256:

An RSA modulus of 2,048 to 8,192 bits, with the public exponent 65,537 (Section 3.3 of [RFC7518]).¶

The key set of an agent or a service is exactly one Ed25519 key followed by one ML-DSA-87 key. A recorder's key set adds one SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s key. A principal's passkey is one ES256, RS256, or Ed25519 key.¶

An Ed25519 key, wherever it appears, MUST NOT encode a y-coordinate greater than or equal to p = 2^255 - 19, and MUST NOT encode the y-coordinate of any of the eight points of small order, whatever the sign bit (bad-key). With a key of small order, anyone can produce a signature that verifies; a y-coordinate of p or more gives one point two encodings. [RFC8032] requires the first check (Section 5.1.3 of [RFC8032]) but not the second, and libraries differ on both, so a verifier performs both itself.¶

A verifier is told which principals it trusts by the thumbprints of their passkeys, and which recorders it trusts by the digests of their key sets. The encoding defines both (Section 11.3).¶

4.5. Hybrid Signatures

A receipt, a countersignature, a delegation, and an acknowledgement carry two signatures over the same content, in this order: first Ed25519 (Section 5.1 of [RFC8032]), verified as Section 4.6 requires; then ML-DSA-87, the pure (not pre-hashed) ML-DSA.Verify of [FIPS204] with an empty context, as Section 5 of [RFC9964] requires. A signed tree head carries a third: SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s, the pure slh_verify of [FIPS205] with an empty context. An empty context is the zero-length string, not a single zero byte. Each signature is over its own signing input, which the encoding defines and which covers the kind of record (Section 11.1). Each is verified with the key at the same position in the applicable key set: the agent's or a service's keys in the permission, a helper's keys in its delegation, or the recorder's keys in the head. Every signature MUST verify; a signature that fails is a problem (signature-invalid).¶

A signature is exactly 64 bytes (Ed25519), 4,627 bytes (ML-DSA-87), or 29,792 bytes (SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s). An Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s signature of any other length is a problem (signature-invalid) on every verifier, whether or not the verifier implements the method. This rule covers every signature of a record, a passkey's Ed25519 signature included; it does not reach a TSA's signature inside a time-stamp (Section 9.3).¶

A signature of the right length whose algorithm the verifier lacks is not verified (Section 10.1). An entry none of whose signatures was verified is not evidence: what it says MUST NOT be shown as fact.¶

4.6. Ed25519 Verification

[RFC8032] leaves some verification choices to implementations, and implementations that choose differently accept different sets of signatures. With public key A, signature halves R and S, message M, base point B, group order L, and n*P for the point P multiplied by the integer n, a verifier MUST:¶

  1. refuse S unless, read as a little-endian integer, it is less than L;¶

  2. refuse A or R unless it decodes to a point on the curve, which includes refusing an encoded y of p or more (Section 5.1.3 of [RFC8032]);¶

  3. refuse A or R of small order, that is, one of the eight points whose order divides 8, including the identity element; and¶

  4. accept only if S*B = R + k*A, where k is the SHA-512 hash of the 32-byte encoding of R, then the 32-byte encoding of A, then M, read as a little-endian integer and reduced modulo L before the multiplication. Comparing the encoding of S*B - k*A with the 32 bytes of R is the same test, since rule 2 makes R's encoding unique. A signature that satisfies only the equation multiplied by the cofactor 8, 8*S*B = 8*R + 8*k*A, is refused.¶

Rules 1 and 2 restate Section 5.1.7 of [RFC8032]; rules 3 and 4 choose where it leaves choices. These rules apply to every Ed25519 signature, a passkey's included; a key whose point is the sum of a point of large order and a point of small order (a mixed-order key) is not refused by these rules.¶

4.7. Passkey Signatures

A permission, an approval, and a cancellation carry one signature by the principal's passkey. To sign, the signer requests an assertion from the passkey with user verification "required". The challenge is the SHA-256 hash of the record's signing input, which the encoding defines and which covers the record's kind and content. The record carries the authenticator data, the client data, and the signature exactly as the passkey returned them (Section 11.1).¶

A verifier checks the signature against the "key", "rpId", and "origin" of the "issuer" of the permission concerned. It follows Section 7.2 of [WebAuthn] as far as it applies to a later check with no state, and MUST confirm that:¶

  1. the client data is UTF-8 with no byte order mark and is a JSON object, read by a JSON parser: white space and member order are not constrained, and a repeated member is read as its last occurrence (passkey-bad-data);¶

  2. its "type" is "webauthn.get" (passkey-wrong-type);¶

  3. its "challenge" is the base64url of the SHA-256 hash of the record's signing input (passkey-challenge-mismatch);¶

  4. its "origin" is the same string as the issuer's, and "crossOrigin" is not the JSON value true (passkey-origin-mismatch); other members are not used;¶

  5. the authenticator data is at least 37 bytes (passkey-bad-data) and begins with the SHA-256 hash of "rpId" (passkey-rpid-mismatch);¶

  6. its flags have "user present" (bit 0) and "user verified" (bit 2) set (passkey-user-not-present, passkey-user-not-verified) and "attested credential data" (bit 6) clear. Without "extension data" (bit 7), the authenticator data is exactly 37 bytes (passkey-bad-data). Other flags and any further bytes are not examined; in particular, the [WebAuthn] rule that "backup state" (bit 4) is clear whenever "backup eligible" (bit 3) is clear is not checked; and¶

  7. the signature verifies over the authenticator data followed by the SHA-256 hash of the client data (signature-invalid). An ES256 signature is an Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA) signature (Section 3.4 of [RFC7518]) stored as an ECDSA-Sig-Value in strict Distinguished Encoding Rules (DER) [X690]: one SEQUENCE with a one-byte length, holding two positive INTEGERs of 1 to 33 bytes in their shortest form, with nothing after them (passkey-bad-data). Either value of s that verifies is accepted. An RS256 signature is as Section 3.3 of [RFC7518].¶

The signature counter, the credential identifier, and the user handle are not used. For an approval or a cancellation, a failure is reported as approval-invalid or cancellation-invalid. If the verifier was given thumbprints of trusted passkeys, the issuer's key MUST be one (issuer-not-expected); if it was given none, it MUST say that the key was not compared with a trusted key. The "rpId" and "origin" of the issuer are part of what a verifier trusts, not only the thumbprint (Section 18).¶

4.8. Records Relied On

An entry that relies on another record names it by its digest. The named record MUST be earlier in the log and sound; otherwise the entry has a problem:¶

  • slip-missing or slip-unusable, for a permission that is missing or not sound;¶

  • pass-missing, for a delegation that is missing or not sound, or pass-mismatch for a delegation under another permission; and¶

  • cancellation-not-found, for a cancellation.¶

A permission or a delegation is judged as it stood when it was itself read. A problem that a later entry adds to it (dated-after-stamp, Section 9.3) changes nothing for the entries that rely on it, though it remains a problem of the log. A cancellation named by an acknowledgement is judged again once the whole log has been read; if it has a problem then, the acknowledgement is reported cancellation-not-found.¶

5. The Permission

A permission's content holds exactly these members, all of them required except "passes":¶

"type":

"provared.slip.v0"¶

"id":

An identifier.¶

"issuer":

The principal: exactly "key", "name", "origin", and "rpId", as specified below.¶

"agent":

Exactly "keys" (a key set) and "name", and optionally "software": 1 to 16 maps {"name", "sha256"}, digests of the program and settings the principal approved. It is a statement, not proof of what ran.¶

"actions":

1 to 64 action names, none repeated.¶

"limits":

0 to 64 limits (Section 5.1).¶

"requires":

0 to 64 conditions (Section 5.1).¶

"never":

0 to 16 names, none repeated (Section 5.1).¶

"with":

0 to 64 services, {"id", "name"} or {"id", "keys", "name"}; "id" has the form of an action name, may begin "provared.", and is unique. A service with no "keys" cannot countersign.¶

"passes":

An integer from 1 to 10 (Section 7).¶

"validFrom", "validUntil":

Times; "validUntil" is later than "validFrom". The permission covers "validFrom" up to, but not including, "validUntil".¶

"purpose":

Text of 1 to 1,000 code points.¶

"rpId" is the WebAuthn Relying Party Identifier (RP ID): 1 to 253 lower-case ASCII letters, digits, ".", and "-", beginning and ending with a letter or a digit.¶

The member "origin" is at most 300 characters and MUST have the following form (bad-field). It is the form in which a browser serializes the origin of a site named by a domain name (Section 6.2 of [RFC6454]), and a verifier can check it without a parser of web addresses and without Unicode tables:¶

  • the scheme, which MUST be "https", or "http" only with the host "localhost";¶

  • "://";¶

  • the host: 1 to 253 characters, made of labels separated by ".", each label 1 to 63 lower-case ASCII letters, digits, and "-", neither beginning nor ending with "-", and the last label not all digits; a name in another script appears in its "xn--" form and is taken as it is; and¶

  • optionally ":" followed by a port, only where the port is not the scheme's default (443 for "https", 80 for "http"), in digits with no leading zero, from 1 to 65,535.¶

Nothing else may follow. The host MUST be "rpId" or end with "." followed by "rpId".¶

An action name is 1 to 64 lower-case ASCII letters, digits, ".", "-", and "_", beginning with a letter or a digit. Names are compared exactly. Names beginning "provared." are reserved for the shared list of Appendix B, and an action name that begins so MUST be on it (bad-field). Any other member is a problem for a core verifier (bad-field); this includes the members by which [FORMAT] covers fields (Section 15).¶

5.1. Limits, Conditions, and Prohibitions

A limit names an "action" from "actions" (bad-field) and holds exactly one of "max", "each", and "count":¶

{"action","max","unit"}        the sum of the amounts is at most "max"
{"action","each","unit"}       no single amount exceeds "each"
{"action","count"}             at most "count" receipts ("count" >= 1)
{"action","max","per","unit"}  as "max", within any period of "per"
                               seconds
{"action","count","per"}       as "count", within any period of "per"
                               seconds
¶

"per" is from 1 to 31,622,400 seconds (366 days). Two limits MUST NOT agree in all three of: "action"; which of "max", "each", or "count" they hold; and "per", or its absence (bad-field). Every "unit" named for one action, in limits and conditions, MUST be the same: a receipt gives one amount.¶

A condition is {"need": "countersignature"} or {"need": "approval"}. It may hold "action", which MUST be one of "actions" (bad-field); without it, the condition applies to every action. The members "above" and "unit" are either both present or both absent, and are present only together with "action"; they restrict the condition to amounts greater than "above" in that unit. Two conditions MUST NOT have the same "need" together with the same "action", or the same "need" and both without "action" (bad-field). The need "countersignature" asks for the service's countersignature (Section 6); "approval" asks for the principal's approval.¶

"never" names kinds of action and rules of conduct from Appendix B. A permission MUST NOT allow, in "actions", a shared action of a kind that its "never" names (bad-field). A rule of conduct is the principal's signed instruction; no check of a record shows that it was kept.¶

6. Action Receipts

A receipt's content holds exactly:¶

"type":

"provared.stub.v0"¶

"id":

An identifier.¶

"slip":

The digest of the permission.¶

"seq":

Its place in its chain, from 0.¶

"previous":

The digest of the receipt before it; present if and only if "seq" is not 0.¶

"action":

An action name.¶

"amount":

Optional: an amount.¶

"with":

Optional: the "id" of a service.¶

"details":

Optional: 1 to 32 maps {"name", "sha256"}, the documents involved.¶

"approval":

Optional: the digest of an approval.¶

"pass":

Optional: the digest of the delegation a helper acts under.¶

"terms":

Optional, with "with": the digest of a service's terms [FORMAT].¶

"when":

The time of the action, as the agent states it.¶

Document names that begin "provared." are reserved. In a receipt's or an approval's "details", a name that begins so MUST be one of the seven names of Section 27 of [FORMAT]: "provared.arguments", "provared.http-request", "provared.http-response", "provared.trace-id", "provared.request-id", "provared.event-id", or "provared.error" (bad-field). Of these, only "provared.trace-id", "provared.request-id", and "provared.event-id" may be named more than once in one record (bad-field). [FORMAT] states the exact text whose digest each reserved name carries; a core verifier checks the names, not the digests.¶

The permission and any delegation named are records relied on (Section 4.8). Both signatures MUST verify with the agent's keys or, for a helper, with the "to" keys of its delegation (signature-invalid). A core verifier accepts no terms entry, so it reports a receipt that holds "terms" as terms-not-found.¶

The receipts of the permission's own agent form one chain, and those under each delegation another. In each chain, in log order, the first has "seq" 0. Each later one has the next "seq" and names the one before in "previous" (chain-broken), and is not dated before it (time-went-backwards). A receipt with a problem keeps its place in its chain, provided its content could be read and the records it relies on were found: the next receipt is checked against it. So one break is reported once.¶

A countersignature has exactly the content {"stub": the receipt's digest, "type": "provared.countersignature.v0", "when": a time}, and states that the action took place with the service. It is carried in the receipt's log entry and signed with the key set the permission gives for the service the receipt names in "with". A verifier MUST confirm "stub" (countersignature-wrong-stub), that such keys exist (countersignature-not-possible), and both signatures (countersignature-invalid). A receipt without a countersignature is one-sided. This is a reported state, not a problem: such a receipt shows only what the agent stated.¶

Where an executor (Section 2) is to countersign, a receipt names the executor in "with", since a countersignature is verified with the keys that the permission gives for the service the receipt names. The party beyond the executor, if there is one, is then not named in "with", and the permission's "with" does not limit which such parties the agent deals with; a reserved document name such as "provared.http-request" can bind the receipt to the call the executor made. Where that party countersigns instead, the receipt names it, and the executor is not named.¶

An approval is the principal's approval of exactly one action. Its content holds exactly "type" ("provared.approval.v0"), "id", "slip", "action", and "when", and optionally "amount", "with", and "details". It is signed with the permission's passkey and carried in the receipt's entry, and the receipt names it by its digest. The receipt MUST name the approval in its entry (approval-mismatch), and an approval it names MUST be there (approval-not-found). The approval's "slip", "action", "amount", "with", and "details" MUST match the receipt's, each present in both or neither (approval-mismatch). Its "id" MUST NOT be that of an approval in an earlier entry (approval-reused) or of another record (duplicate-id). Its passkey signature MUST verify against the permission's "issuer" (approval-invalid). It MUST NOT be dated more than 300 seconds after the receipt, which was signed after it (approval-dated-after-stub). An approval shows that the passkey approved the action, not that the principal read it.¶

7. Delegation

"passes" says how many times in a row a permission may be delegated: 1 lets its agent delegate to a helper, 2 lets that helper delegate once more, up to 10. Without it, a permission may not be delegated. A delegation's content holds exactly:¶

"type":

"provared.pass.v0"¶

"id":

An identifier.¶

"slip":

The permission's digest.¶

"from":

The digest of the delegation its writer acts under; absent when the writer is the permission's own agent.¶

"to":

The helper: exactly {"keys": key set, "name": name}.¶

"actions":

As in a permission.¶

"limits":

As in a permission, naming only these "actions".¶

"validFrom", "validUntil":

Its validity, as in a permission.¶

"when":

When it was written.¶

It is signed by its writer: with the agent's keys or, with "from", the "to" keys of that delegation (signature-invalid). The permission and the delegation in "from" are records relied on (Section 4.8). Its depth is 1 without "from", otherwise one more than that of "from"; a depth above 10 is a problem (pass-too-deep).¶

A verifier reports these findings for a delegation:¶

  • pass-not-allowed, if its depth exceeds "passes" (taken as 0 when "passes" is absent);¶

  • pass-wider, if any of its "actions" is not among its writer's, or its validity begins earlier or ends later than its writer's;¶

  • outside-valid-time, if "when" is outside its writer's validity; and¶

  • after-cancellation, as Section 8 sets out.¶

"Its writer's" means the permission's, or, with "from", the delegation's named there.¶

A receipt under a delegation is compared with the permission exactly as the agent's own receipts are, and shares their totals, conditions, and "never". It is also compared with its own delegation and with every delegation above it, each treated as a permission for its "actions", "limits", and validity; the totals of a delegation include everything done under delegations from it. A finding code already reported for a receipt is not repeated. Every receipt under a delegation reported pass-not-allowed, or under a delegation below it, is reported pass-not-allowed. Because the limits of the permission and of every delegation above always apply, a delegation never widens what the verifier accepts as within the permission.¶

8. Cancellation and Acknowledgement

A cancellation has exactly the content {"id", "slip", "type": "provared.cancellation.v0", "when"}, and is signed with the permission's passkey and verified against its "issuer" (cancellation-invalid); the permission is a record relied on (Section 4.8). Its entry may hold "stamps" over its digest (Section 9.3), which the principal can obtain at once.¶

The effective cancellation of a permission is the first cancellation of it in the log that is sound when read and whose passkey signature, and that of its permission, were verified. Every receipt and delegation under the permission that follows the effective cancellation in the log is reported after-cancellation.¶

Section 22 of [FORMAT] adds a stricter rule for a time-stamped cancellation. A receipt before it in the log is also reported, unless a time-stamp on an earlier head shows that the receipt existed by the cancellation's time, within 300 seconds. This document does not specify that rule (Section 16). Without that rule, a core verifier can miss receipts made after a cancellation.¶

A cancellation does not show that the agent was told; an acknowledgement is the agent's signed statement that it was. Its content holds exactly "cancellation" (a digest), "id", "slip", "type" ("provared.acknowledgement.v0"), and "when", and optionally "pass". It is signed with the agent's keys or a helper's (signature-invalid). The permission, any delegation named, and the cancellation, which MUST cancel the same permission (cancellation-not-found), are records relied on (Section 4.8). An acknowledgement changes no finding, and its "when" is not compared with the cancellation's; its signer SHOULD NOT date it before the last receipt or delegation its agent wrote. Section 26 of [FORMAT] sets out how a verifier uses it with the principal's own copy.¶

9. Logs, Signed Tree Heads, and Time-Stamps

9.1. The Log

A log is a sequence of entries, numbered from 0. Each entry holds one record, with what is carried beside it: a permission; an action receipt, optionally with a countersignature and an approval; a delegation; a cancellation, optionally with time-stamps; an acknowledgement; or a signed tree head, optionally with time-stamps. Any other entry is a problem (unknown-entry). A core verifier reports the four further kinds of [FORMAT] ("refusal", "terms", "vouching", "withdrawal") in the same way, and so never calls such a log intact. The same permission MUST NOT appear twice (duplicate-slip), nor the same delegation (duplicate-id). A log MUST NOT exceed 100,000 entries (too-large). Section 11.4 gives the form of a log in the JSON encoding.¶

The leaves of the tree are the entries as the encoding writes them, in log order; the root is the Merkle Tree Hash of Section 2.1.1 of [RFC9162] with SHA-256. Inclusion and consistency proofs are checked against a size as well as a root, so the two are kept together. A verifier given a root or a size to expect MUST compare it (root-mismatch).¶

9.2. Signed Tree Heads

A signed tree head's content holds exactly "type" ("provared.seal.v0"), "id", "by" (exactly {"keys": the recorder's key set, "name"}), "size" (at least 1), "root", "previous" (the previous head's digest, absent for the first), and "when". A head is signed with the three keys in "by" and appended as the next entry, so each head covers those before it. A verifier holding the whole log MUST confirm that:¶

  1. the three signatures verify (signature-invalid);¶

  2. "size" is its own place in the log, and "root" the root of the entries before it (seal-mismatch);¶

  3. "previous" names the nearest earlier head, or is absent if there is none (seal-chain-broken);¶

  4. "when" is not before that head's (time-went-backwards); and¶

  5. if given digests of trusted recorders' key sets, that of "by.keys" is one (sealer-not-expected); if given none, it MUST say that the keys were not compared with trusted keys.¶

Section 21 of [FORMAT] adds rules that compare a head's "when" with the times its entries state and with earlier heads' time-stamps (time-went-backwards). This document does not specify them (Section 16).¶

9.3. Time-Stamps

"stamps" holds 1 to 4 time-stamps, none repeated. A time-stamp is either an [RFC3161] TimeStampToken or a block time-stamp of [FORMAT]. A TimeStampToken is at most 12,288 bytes; its "messageImprint" has the hashAlgorithm SHA-256 and a hashedMessage equal to the 32 bytes of the record's digest, not hashed again. A block time-stamp holds exactly "block" and "proof", both bytes (Section 11.4); a core verifier does not count it, and so answers no to the second question of Section 10.1. Time-stamps are read only if none of the record's own signatures failed. A verifier MUST confirm that:¶

  1. the token is one element with nothing after it, laid out as [RFC3161] and [RFC5652] require: signed data of version 3 with exactly one signer, of version 1 or 3, enclosing a TSTInfo of version 1 (stamp-bad-data);¶

  2. it meets these rules of DER [X690] (stamp-bad-data):¶

    • definite lengths in their shortest form, with a length field of at most four bytes, and single-byte tags;¶

    • every constructed element exactly filled by its parts;¶

    • every INTEGER and OBJECT IDENTIFIER in its shortest form; every BOOLEAN 0x00 or 0xFF; every NULL empty; every BIT STRING with at most 7 unused bits;¶

    • genTime and each certificate's validity times in the one form DER gives them, denoting a moment that exists;¶

    • the public key of the signing certificate written exactly as its own specification sets out, with no unused bits: RSA as Section 2.3.1 of [RFC3279], with the parameters present and NULL and two positive INTEGERs with nothing after them; ECDSA as Section 2.2 of [RFC5480], as an uncompressed point; Ed25519 as Section 4 of [RFC8410], with no parameters and 32 bytes; and¶

    • at most 4,000 elements, none more than 24 levels deep, in each of the signed data, the enclosed TSTInfo (read from its OCTET STRING), and an RSA public key; at most 8 digest algorithms, 8 certificates, 16 signed attributes, 4 values in one attribute, 8 identifiers in a signing-certificate attribute, and 16 TSTInfo extensions.¶

    The order of SET OF elements and the omission of DEFAULT values are not checked;¶

  3. the "messageImprint" is as above (stamp-wrong-data);¶

  4. the signed attributes hold a content type of id-ct-TSTInfo, and the TSTInfo's message digest made with the signer's digest algorithm (SHA-256, SHA-384, or SHA-512). They also hold ESSCertIDv2 ([RFC5035], [RFC5816]), with SHA-256 (the default), SHA-384, or SHA-512, or ESSCertID ([RFC2634]), with SHA-1, ESSCertIDv2 being used if both are present; the first identifier in the attribute names the certificate, which the token carries; the issuerSerial is not compared. Each of these attributes appears at most once and holds exactly one value, as [RFC5652] and [RFC5035] require (stamp-invalid);¶

  5. the signature over the signed attributes verifies with that certificate's key (stamp-invalid). The signature algorithm names the signer's digest algorithm (for RSA, rsaEncryption is also accepted). The accepted methods are RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5 with a modulus of 2,048 to 8,192 bits and an odd public exponent from 3 to 2^32 - 1 (with an exponent of 1, anyone can produce a signature that verifies); ECDSA on P-256 or P-384; and Ed25519 [RFC8419]. An Ed25519 key that Section 4.4 refuses, or a key of any other kind or on any other curve, is refused (stamp-invalid); and¶

  6. "genTime" lies within the certificate's validity, the seconds field of any "accuracy" is at most 300, and no TSTInfo extension is critical (stamp-invalid). The certificate's extensions are not examined, since the certificate is trusted by its digest.¶

A verifier is told which TSAs it trusts by the digest of each TSA's certificate in DER; it builds no chain, checks no revocation, and asks no outside party. A time-stamp counts only if its TSA is trusted and every signature of its record was verified (for a cancellation, also its permission's). A sound time-stamp from a TSA not trusted MUST be shown as such, with its certificate's digest; a time-stamp that does not count MUST NOT be used as a time. Times are taken to the second, rounded down.¶

A counted time-stamp at time T shows that the record existed by T. On a sound signed tree head, it also shows that every entry before the head existed by T. An entry existed by the earliest such T of any later sound head. A verifier MUST report "existed by", never "existed at", and MUST say which entries the last counted time-stamp reaches. It MUST report dated-after-stamp for a head or a cancellation whose "when" is more than 300 seconds after the earliest time its counted time-stamps state, and for an entry whose "when", or the "when" of a countersignature or approval carried in it, is more than 300 seconds after the time by which the entry existed.¶

10. Verification

10.1. The Three Answers

A verifier is given the log and, optionally, the size and root it expects, the thumbprints of the passkeys it trusts, and the digests of the key sets of the recorders and of the certificates of the TSAs it trusts. It makes no network request. It gives three answers, kept apart:¶

  1. Was a problem found? Yes if any entry, or the log, has one.¶

  2. Was everything verified? Yes only if every entry was read as far as its signatures, and no signature or time-stamp in the log was left unverified because the verifier does not implement its method.¶

  3. Did each action stay within its permission? Yes only if no problem was found, every receipt and delegation was compared, and none gave a finding.¶

A verifier MUST call a log intact only if no problem was found and everything was verified. A receipt or delegation is compared only if it is sound, at least one of its signatures was verified and none failed, and its permission's passkey signature was verified. So the third answer can be yes while the second is no: for example, a verifier without ML-DSA-87 compares receipts on their Ed25519 signatures alone. When the second answer is no, a verifier MUST say that the third rests only on the signatures it verified, and MUST name the signing methods it lacked.¶

A verifier checks each record's encoding (in the JSON encoding, its envelope, labels, and content, Section 11), then its members, and then finds the records it relies on, in that order. At the first problem in those steps, it reports that problem's code and checks nothing further in that entry. Once an entry's members have been read, and before the records it relies on are looked up, its "id" is compared with the identifiers seen so far and then recorded; an approval's "id" is compared and recorded once the approval has been matched to its receipt. Every other problem of an entry (a signature, its place in its chain, a countersignature, an approval, a repeated "id") is reported. Anything the verifier did not expect is a problem (check-failed), never a pass. Each problem and finding is reported with its entry's number. Two verifiers agree if they give the same three answers, find problems at the same entries, and report the same findings for each entry.¶

10.2. Findings

A verifier reports these findings at the receipt concerned:¶

action-not-allowed:

"action" is not in "actions".¶

prohibited:

"action" is a shared action of a kind that "never" names.¶

party-not-allowed:

"with" names no service of the permission.¶

outside-valid-time:

"when" is outside the permission's validity.¶

amount-missing:

A limit with a "unit", or a condition with "above", applies to the action, and the receipt has no amount in that unit (once a receipt).¶

over-limit, over-each-limit, over-count-limit:

The running total exceeds "max"; the amount exceeds "each"; with this receipt, the number exceeds "count".¶

over-period-limit:

Within the period ending at this receipt, the total exceeds "max" or the number "count".¶

countersignature-missing, approval-missing:

A condition applies, and the entry holds no countersignature, or no approval.¶

after-cancellation, pass-not-allowed:

As Section 8 and Section 7 set out.¶

A condition with "above" applies only if the amount is in its unit and greater than "above"; an amount missing or in another unit is amount-missing. Only compared receipts count toward totals.¶

Totals and counts without "per" accumulate in log order over every compared receipt for the action under the permission, across all chains including the current one. Once a total is exceeded, every later receipt for that action is reported too. A limit with "per" P is evaluated for each compared receipt X, dated t, over the period that ends at X. That period holds: every receipt for the action, in any chain and anywhere in the log, dated later than t - P and earlier than t; those dated exactly t that precede X in the log; and X itself. Because a later entry may state an earlier time, period limits are settled only once every receipt has been read. Periods are counted in seconds; there are no calendar days and no time zones.¶

11. The JSON Encoding

This section specifies the JSON encoding: how records, keys, and logs are written as JSON [RFC8259], and how signatures are carried, with JWS [RFC7515].¶

11.1. Envelope and Labels

Every record is a JWS in the General JWS JSON Serialization (Section 7.2.1 of [RFC7515]). It MUST hold exactly the members "payload" and "signatures". Each element of "signatures" MUST hold exactly "protected" and "signature" and, for a passkey signature, also "header", which holds exactly "authenticatorData" and "clientDataJSON" (bad-envelope). A passkey's authenticator data and client data are stored there, and its signature in "signature", each exactly as returned. The signing input of every signature is the JWS Signing Input (Section 5.1 of [RFC7515]) formed from "protected" and "payload" as carried.¶

Every value written in base64url, in a record's envelope or content or in a log entry, is that of Section 5 of [RFC4648], without padding, and is read strictly: a verifier MUST refuse padding, a character outside the alphabet, a length that leaves 1 when divided by 4, and unused bits that are not zero (bad-base64url; in a key, bad-key; in "authenticatorData" and "clientDataJSON", passkey-bad-data). The decoded "signature" of an Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s signature MUST have its method's length (Section 4.5; signature-invalid).¶

The protected header of each signature, once decoded, MUST be exactly the UTF-8 bytes of {"typ":"<typ>","alg":"<alg>"}, with no white space and the two members in that order, where <typ> and <alg> are as follows. This header is a fixed text, compared as bytes; it is deliberately not in the canonical member order of Section 11.2. A verifier compares bytes and interprets nothing else. A header that is not a known text is a problem (unknown-type). One that belongs to another kind of record than the one expected is a problem too (payload-type-mismatch). The number and order of the signatures are those of Section 4.1 (bad-signatures-layout).¶

<typ> is "vnd.provared.X.v0+json", with X as in Section 4.1: the subtype of the record's media type, written without its "application/" prefix as Section 4.1.9 of [RFC7515] allows. The placeholder <alg> names the method of the signature: "Ed25519", "ML-DSA-87", "SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s", or "prova.red/webauthn/v0" for a passkey. A passkey's signature is its assertion signature over the authenticator data and the client data (Section 4.7); the JWS Signing Input enters it only through the challenge. The "typ" value is used for explicit typing (Section 3.11 of [RFC8725]); since it is signed, every signature covers the kind of record. The name "Ed25519" is registered by [RFC9864] and "ML-DSA-87" by [RFC9964]. The name "prova.red/webauthn/v0" is collision-resistant (Section 4.1.1 of [RFC7515]) and is not registered. The name "SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s", which [FIPS205] gives the parameter set, is neither registered nor collision-resistant, one of which Section 4.1.1 of [RFC7515] says an "alg" value should be; this document fixes it as a private-use value, compared byte for byte. It follows the naming of [I-D.ietf-cose-sphincs-plus], which registers the 128s parameter sets with the same key form (AKP), pure mode, and the empty context, so that a registration of this name with that meaning would be compatible.¶

11.2. Content and Values

The content of a record is the decoded "payload", and its digest is the SHA-256 hash of those bytes. It MUST be a JSON object in UTF-8, in the canonical form of [RFC8785]: a verifier serializes it again and refuses it if a single byte differs (payload-not-canonical). This refuses repeated member names, white space, and members out of order. The content MUST hold only objects, arrays, strings, and integers from 0 to 2^53 - 1. It holds no "true", "false", "null", fractions, exponents, or negative numbers, and no unpaired surrogate, even escaped. Objects and arrays MUST NOT nest more than 8 levels deep, the content itself being the first (payload-not-canonical). The signed bytes are verified as signed; the canonical form is a test, never a rewrite.¶

A map is written as an object, a list as an array, text as a string, and an integer as a number. The values of Section 4.3 are written so:¶

Bytes:

In base64url, read strictly as Section 11.1 sets out.¶

Time:

"YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ", an [RFC3339] date-time (bad-field).¶

Key:

A JSON Web Key (JWK) [RFC7517] of exactly one of the shapes below, with no other member (bad-key).¶

{"alg":"Ed25519","crv":"Ed25519","kty":"OKP","x":X}     X: 32 bytes
{"alg":"ML-DSA-87","kty":"AKP","pub":P}               P: 2592 bytes
{"alg":"SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s","kty":"AKP","pub":P}         P: 64 bytes
{"alg":"ES256","crv":"P-256","kty":"EC","x":X,"y":Y}  X, Y: 32 bytes
{"alg":"RS256","e":"AQAB","kty":"RSA","n":N}     N: 2048-8192 bits
¶

These follow [RFC8037] (the Octet Key Pair, "OKP", key type), Section 3 of [RFC9964] (the Algorithm Key Pair, "AKP", key type), and Section 6 of [RFC7518]; "n" has no leading zero byte. A key set is an array of keys.¶

A record MUST NOT exceed 65,536 characters, counted as the length of "payload" plus the lengths of every string in its signature entries, "header" included (too-large).¶

11.3. Thumbprints and Key-Set Digests

A passkey is named by its key thumbprint: [RFC7638] with SHA-256, over the members required by Section 3.2 of [RFC7638], Section 2 of [RFC8037] for "OKP", and Section 6 of [RFC9964] for "AKP". A recorder is named by the digest of its key set: the SHA-256 hash of the key set written in the canonical form of Section 11.2.¶

11.4. Logs

A log is UTF-8 text, one entry to a line, each line ended by a line feed except perhaps the last. A log with no entry, an empty line, or a byte order mark is a problem (not-json); so is a line ending in a carriage return (line-not-canonical). Each line is a JSON object in the canonical form of Section 11.2, with records as values (line-not-canonical). It holds exactly the members of one kind of entry (Section 9.1): "slip" (a permission); "stub", optionally with "countersignature" and "approval" (an action receipt); "pass" (a delegation); "cancellation", optionally with "stamps"; "acknowledgement"; or "seal" (a signed tree head), optionally with "stamps" (unknown-entry). A line MUST NOT exceed 131,072 bytes (too-large). The leaves of the tree are the lines' bytes, without line feeds.¶

In "stamps", a TimeStampToken is a string, in base64url. A block time-stamp is an object of exactly "block" and "proof", each a string (bad-field) in base64url (Section 11.1).¶

12. Other Encodings

Another encoding could carry the same content model: for example, one using COSE_Sign (Section 4.1 of [RFC9052]) over deterministically encoded CBOR (Section 4.2 of [RFC8949]). This document does not define one. Such an encoding has to define:¶

  1. the bytes of a record's content, in one form that a verifier tests, so that a record's digest is fixed; it may write member names in another fixed form, such as short integer labels;¶

  2. how the signatures are carried, in the number and order of Section 4.1, and the signing input of each, which covers the kind of record (with COSE, perhaps by the "typ" header parameter [RFC9596]) and its content;¶

  3. how each signature names its method, in fixed bytes that a verifier compares: with COSE, the "alg" of each signer, which has values for Ed25519 [RFC9864] and ML-DSA-87 [RFC9964], but none yet for SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s or the passkey signature;¶

  4. the passkey's challenge, the SHA-256 hash of that signing input, and where the authenticator data and client data are stored;¶

  5. how bytes, times, and keys are written (with COSE, keys as COSE_Key), and the thumbprints ([RFC9679]) and key-set digests by which a verifier is told whom it trusts;¶

  6. the form of a log and of its entries, which are the leaves of the tree; and¶

  7. its size limits, and the codes for problems of its own form.¶

The rest of the content model, the three answers, and the findings apply unchanged. A record's digest depends on its encoding, so records that name one another, and the entries of one log, are in one encoding. The thumbprints and key-set digests by which a verifier is told whom it trusts also depend on the encoding: a key's JWK thumbprint and its COSE_Key thumbprint differ.¶

13. Relationship to Other Work

Agent receipts:

[I-D.nelson-agent-delegation-receipts] has the user sign an authorization (WebAuthn being one way) with allowed and denied actions, prohibitions, and a time window, logged before the agent acts; its delegations may only narrow, within a depth limit, and can be revoked. [I-D.sahu-agent-action-receipts] and [I-D.dembowski-agentledger-proof-of-behavior] (which expired on 22 October 2026) specify signed, hash-chained action receipts, with a recorded policy decision or a gate run before execution. Here the permission carries limits on amounts, counts, and periods, and anyone can compare each recorded action with it afterward.¶

Audit records:

[I-D.gilda-audit-agent-audit-record] records an authorization decision and the effect that followed, as one in-toto predicate in a DSSE envelope, with a canonicalization contract and what a retaining party keeps beside a record so that it can later be dated from anchors it does not control; it defines no policy. Where a decision applied a cumulative limit set by a document named by digest, the record states the limit and the amounts that the decision point counted as committed, held, and requested; a verifier derives from them whether the decision was within the limit, and a party that holds every record under one limit checks them against one another (Section 5.9 of [I-D.gilda-audit-agent-audit-record]). [I-D.sharif-agent-audit-trail] specifies a JSON logging record with mandatory fields for the agent, the authorizing principal, the action's class, and its outcome, hash-chained with SHA-256 over [RFC8785] and optionally signed with ECDSA P-256, ML-DSA-65, or Ed25519, or with ECDSA P-256 and ML-DSA-65 side by side; it requires a denial or an escalation to be recorded before execution, recommends a recorder independent of the agent at its higher trust levels, and maps the record to regulatory logging requirements. [I-D.sergeev-audit-task-requirements] states which evidence answers which audit question and separates missing evidence from a negative observation; the three answers of Section 10.1 and the omission limits of Section 18 are of that kind. None has the principal sign the limits against which a verifier compares each recorded action: the first checks a cumulative amount against the amounts that the decision point recorded, not against the actions' own signed records, and has no limits on counts, single amounts, or periods. The permission here could be the document that sets such a limit, or the authorization under which a decision recorded by the first two was made.¶

EMILIA Protocol receipts:

In [I-D.schrock-ep-authorization-receipts], an enrolled approver, never the initiator, signs a context bound to one exact action before it is executed, preferably with a WebAuthn credential whose challenge is the hash of that context, with user verification required. The executor records one-time use in shared atomic state; the receipt, which carries a log inclusion proof, can be verified offline. In [I-D.schrock-ep-bounded-capability-receipts], an issuer signs with Ed25519 a capability holding a closed scope, a budget in explicit units, an expiry, and any parent; its issuance can itself be approved by such a receipt. Delegation only narrows, and is funded from the parent's budget. An overspend or a replayed operation is refused when an operation is reserved, and revocation is checked then and again just before the action takes effect, within one atomic store. That document states that it provides no offline or cross-domain double-spend prevention, and that a committed operation is not verifiable evidence unless a signed record covers it; Section 13 of that document lists what such evidence should hold. Together, the two EMILIA documents provide enforcement before an action takes effect, in the executor's atomic state, and evidence of what was authorized, in the authorization receipt. An approval here (Section 6) corresponds to the first document, and a signed total with narrowing delegation to the second. The difference is where the limits live and who checks them: there, the executor's store refuses an operation; here, the principal's passkey signs the limits, which may also bound single amounts, counts, and periods and set conditions, each action is a signed receipt that the service may countersign, and any verifier decides afterward, from the records alone, whether each action stayed within the permission. This document does not stop an action when it is attempted, and an action for which no receipt was written is found only in another party's records (Section 18). The two designs could be combined: an executor (Section 2) that refuses an operation before it takes effect, and receipts from which any verifier can later check each action against the limits the principal signed.¶

SCITT profiles:

A SCITT Transparency Service [RFC9943] registers Signed Statements and returns Receipts [RFC9942] proving inclusion in its log; a SCITT Receipt proves registration, not an action. [I-D.noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt] records a principal class and an ALLOW or DENY verdict, keeps pre-execution authorization apart, and does not claim that a named approver authorized the action. [I-D.emirdag-scitt-ai-agent-execution] has the operator sign each record, with an independent custodian as Transparency Service; a principal's constraints appear only as an optional text field and references to credentials. [I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule] records one action per capsule, with its disposition and the constraints evaluated, and leaves permission to authorization records it references by digest. None defines a permission signed by the principal, with limits that a verifier compares receipts against. That content model could be carried by a SCITT profile, with heads or receipts registered as Signed Statements, also guarding against truncation and split views (Section 18).¶

OAuth and GNAP:

Token Exchange expresses delegation through the "act" claim (Section 4.1 of [RFC8693]); Rich Authorization Requests [RFC9396] and the Grant Negotiation and Authorization Protocol (GNAP) [RFC9635] grant detailed rights. All yield tokens issued by a server for access, not records signed by the principal. Transaction Tokens [I-D.ietf-oauth-transaction-tokens] carry one call's context through a trust domain; a receipt can hold a token's digest in "details".¶

Attenuating tokens:

[I-D.niyikiza-oauth-attenuating-agent-tokens] lets a holder derive offline tokens with equal or narrower capabilities, within depth and lifetime limits, checked before each call; macaroons [MACAROONS], Biscuit [BISCUIT], and User Controlled Authorization Network (UCAN) [UCAN] narrow capabilities likewise. Delegation here also only narrows, but is checked over recorded actions, with totals and periods across many of them.¶

WIMSE:

Workload Identity in a Multi System Environment (WIMSE) [I-D.ietf-wimse-arch] gives workloads identities; [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims] applies WIMSE and OAuth to AI agents. A WIMSE credential for an agent's keys would say which workload held them.¶

Credentials and mandates:

A permission resembles a credential ([VC-DATA-MODEL], [I-D.ietf-oauth-sd-jwt-vc]) and a mandate of the Agent Payments Protocol [AP2], by which a user authorizes purchases within limits; but it is checked against receipts, and handles no payment.¶

Agent auditing:

The approved request for a working-group-forming session on Agent Use of Delegation and Interaction Traceability (AUDIT) at IETF 127 [AUDIT-BOFREQ], dated 8 October 2026, lists [I-D.kuehlewind-audit-architecture] and [I-D.birkholz-verifiable-agent-conversations] as its architecture and solution drafts, and a data model for records among the new work required; at the time of writing (October 2026) the proposed group's charter is still being revised. The architecture's first work item leaves the encoding open, CBOR and COSE or JSON and JWS, with detached payloads. In its terms, a permission records an initial grant, an approval a step-up approval, and a cancellation a revocation, which are kinds of Authorization Transition Record; a delegation is a Delegation Record. It places Action Records where an action took effect and binds each to its authorizing token; here the agent's receipt names its permission by digest, and the countersignature comes from the side where the action took effect. Its records share a correlation identifier, which this document does not define (Section 16). This document offers the permission with limits and the comparison rule as content that any record format in that work could carry.¶

14. Design Rationale

Passkeys are on the devices people use, resist phishing, and record user verification; no device offers a post-quantum passkey. The JSON encoding is defined first because an implementation uses it, and JSON text suits a log of one entry to a line. Two signatures side by side can each be verified by any library, and a verifier lacking one says so. Composite signatures [I-D.ietf-jose-pq-composite-sigs] would make one of the two, but that draft pairs ML-DSA-87 only with ES384 or Ed448, never with Ed25519. Verification needs no network, because a record may be checked years later, when its writers have gone. Post-quantum signatures are used from the start: if Ed25519 is forged in future, a record carrying only Ed25519 could be made afterward and not told apart from a genuine one; SLH-DSA rests on hash functions alone. The tree is that of Certificate Transparency, the covering of fields in [FORMAT] that of [RFC9901], and the time-stamp that of the Time-Stamp Protocol.¶

15. Parts Specified Elsewhere

[FORMAT] specifies these parts, out of scope here: one-page proofs of single entries, with a permission's names and purpose covered by the disclosures of [RFC9901]; vouching records, by which an organization states that a key belongs to a name, and their withdrawal; a service's signed refusals and terms; time-stamps from a public blockchain; further date rules; the principal's own copy of a cancellation, handed to a verifier; the exact texts whose digests the reserved document names of Section 6 carry (Section 27 of [FORMAT]), which bind a receipt to a web request, an answer, a call's arguments, an identifier for the call from either side, or an error text; and the check before acting (Section 20 of [FORMAT]), by which the software around an agent asks, before an action, whether it would stay within the permission. How records are carried, how keys are distributed, and what to do about a finding are out of scope too.¶

18. Security Considerations

Making or widening a permission requires a signature by the principal's passkey; making or changing any other record requires its signer's keys. Without the keys that signed a chain, a receipt cannot be removed from the middle of the chain, moved within it, or changed without a verifier reporting it (chain-broken, signature-invalid). Whoever holds those keys can re-sign the chain from any receipt onward. Anyone can remove the last receipts of a chain, unless a head held by someone other than the recorder covers them, or the service has kept a countersignature of one of them. A verifier that holds a copy of a signed tree head, or of its size and root, obtained from someone other than the recorder, detects any change, addition, or removal before that head (root-mismatch). Trusting the recorder's keys protects against everyone except the recorder, who can sign a shortened or reordered history. A counted time-stamp shows that the entries before a sound head existed by its time. Once a permission is covered by a head whose keys the verifier trusts, two of the head's three signatures, which are post-quantum, cover the permission's entry. The passkey signature on the permission itself is classical. What the head adds is that the permission, as it stands in the log, cannot be replaced afterward by anyone without the recorder's keys, even if its passkey signature later becomes forgeable; it does not make the principal's signature post-quantum.¶

What this document does not protect against:¶

No trusted keys:

With no trusted keys named, "intact" means only that the log is consistent with itself. Anyone can make such a log.¶

Omission, one side's word:

An agent that writes no receipt leaves no trace in its own log, and no check of that log shows the gap. Only another party's records can show it: a countersignature kept by the service, or the records of an executor (Section 2) that refuses any action the permission does not allow, or any action without a verified approval, and records each action it allows (the strongest class of [I-D.schrock-ep-authorization-receipts] has such an executor, which checks that document's own authorizations). An executor's records show the gap only if the action cannot take place without the executor, and if the executor keeps them where the agent's side cannot change them. A permission whose "with" names no service but the executor, and that requires a countersignature for every action, makes each receipt in the log that lacks the executor's countersignature a finding (countersignature-missing, or party-not-allowed for a receipt that names another party). A receipt that was never written is found only by comparing the executor's own records with the log, which this document does not specify. A cancellation the principal signed can be left out of the log by whoever keeps it, after which every later receipt reads as within the permission; only the principal's own copy of the cancellation, handed to the verifier (Section 22 of [FORMAT], out of scope here), answers this. A one-sided receipt, an acknowledgement, and the times stated in records, on which period limits depend, rest on the signer's own statement.¶

Truncation and split views:

The last head, with everything after it, can be removed unnoticed unless someone else holds that head. A recorder can keep two logs that differ after some entry, with heads for each; no consistency proof is defined. Handing heads to others lets them be compared.¶

One log only:

approval-reused and duplicate-id hold within one log, and so do totals, counts, and periods. Receipts for one permission written into two logs, or into a log that was started afresh, are not added together: each log can be within its limits while the two together are not. A recorder that keeps one log for each permission avoids this; the format does not close it.¶

Service keys:

The keys of a service in "with" are stated by the principal. A countersignature shows that a holder of those keys signed; it does not show who that holder is. A principal who lists keys they control can countersign their own agent's receipts. A compromise of a service's keys lets anyone countersign in its name: a countersignature is only as strong as the service's keys.¶

Agent keys held by the recorder:

Such a recorder can write and sign another history. A time-stamp then shows only when; countersignatures still stand against it.¶

Passkeys:

A passkey shows that a device signed after verifying a user, not who held it, nor that the principal read what was signed; a compromised device or page can show one thing and have another signed. The challenge is opaque to the principal. Any party that can choose the challenge at an origin under the issuer's "rpId" (the site's operator, any script at that origin, or any subdomain of the "rpId") can obtain a signature on a record of its choosing by presenting it as a sign-in: the client data of a sign-in and of a record are the same shape. So a passkey trusted for records SHOULD be created for an "rpId" used for nothing but signing records, the signing page SHOULD show the record before asking, and a verifier MAY be told the "origin" it expects as well as the thumbprint. The counter is not used, so a cloned passkey goes unseen. An "http://localhost" origin is for tests only.¶

Time-stamp keys:

A TSA is trusted by certificate digest, without revocation, and its time is checked against the certificate's validity, not the verifier's clock. A compromised TSA key can stamp any time; a verifier that learns of it MUST stop trusting it. Up to four TSAs lessen the dependence on one.¶

Renewal:

TSA signatures are classical, and certificates age; so the times by which entries existed rest on classical signatures and have no post-quantum protection. A later head covers earlier heads' entries, time-stamps included, so stamping it by a newer TSA extends the evidence, as evidence records [RFC4998] do.¶

Stripping:

Signatures are fixed in number, order, and label, and their keys are given elsewhere (Section 4.5), so removing or replacing one is caught.¶

Ed25519 alone:

Without ML-DSA-87, what is compared rests on Ed25519 alone. Such a verifier cannot tell a record made with the agent's Ed25519 key alone from a genuine one. A verifier whose library applies other rules than Section 4.6 MUST add the missing checks.¶

Malleable signatures:

An ECDSA signature can be altered into another valid one. A record digest covers only content, so no digest changes; the entry does, and a later head shows it.¶

A verifier MUST treat every byte of a record as untrusted, and answer a failed check with a code, not a crash. It SHOULD replace control, invisible, and direction-changing characters before showing text from a record. Member names are plain strings: "__proto__" is like any other.¶

19. Privacy Considerations

Following [RFC6973]: keys repeat in every record a party signs, so records in different logs can be linked. Document digests are not salted, so whoever can guess a document can confirm that a receipt names it; a writer can add a random value to a document first. A TSA learns the digests it stamps, when, and who asked. An entry cannot be erased without breaking later heads, so records should hold no more personal data than needed; names may be pseudonyms, and [FORMAT] defines covered names and purpose. The members "origin" and "rpId" name the site where the principal signed. A verifier makes no network request.¶

Appendix B. Shared List

Names that begin "provared." are reserved for this list; [FORMAT] also gives each action's meaning. Shared action names are written below without their "provared." prefix: "data.read" stands for "provared.data.read". Kinds and rules of conduct are written as shown, with no prefix; a "never" list may name any of them.¶

"reads":

Looks at data without changing it. Actions: "data.read", "data.search", "message.read", "calendar.read", "code.read", "web.read".¶

"changes":

Creates or changes data in a way that can be put back. Actions: "data.create", "data.change", "message.draft", "calendar.change", "access.remove", "code.change", "system.configure".¶

"destroys":

Removes data or a resource in a way that may not be put back. Actions: "data.delete".¶

"sends":

Sends a message or data to a person, to the public, or to another system. Actions: "data.export", "message.send", "post.publish", "agent.instruct".¶

"commits":

Binds the principal to something: an order, a booking, an agreement, an account. Actions: "booking.make", "booking.cancel", "order.place", "order.cancel", "terms.accept", "form.submit", "account.create".¶

"grants":

Gives someone or something access or permission. Actions: "access.grant", "key.create".¶

"runs":

Runs a program or a command. Actions: "code.run", "code.release".¶

"enters":

Signs in to a system or an account. Actions: "account.sign-in".¶

"impersonate":

Never claim to be a human being, or to be anyone other than the agent of the principal who signed the permission.¶

"bypass":

Never go around an access control, a block, or a refusal.¶

"escalate":

Never obtain or use access that the agent was not given.¶

"conceal":

Never hide, alter, or leave out the agent's own records.¶

"deceive":

Never state what the agent has reason to believe is false, and never make up a record or a result.¶

"harass":

Never threaten, pressure, or attack a person.¶

"disclose":

Never pass information that is not public to anyone the permission does not name.¶

"continue-after-stop":

Never carry on after being told to stop.¶

Appendix D. Changes Since -00

This section is to be removed before publishing as an RFC.¶

  • The content model (Section 4 through Section 10) is specified apart from its encoding. The JWS form is now Section 11, with its rules unchanged; Section 12 states what another encoding, such as one with COSE, has to define. Log "lines" are now "entries" outside Section 11, and the codes in Appendix A are grouped by where they arise.¶

  • No record's bytes change, but a verifier of -01 refuses some records that -00 accepted, to match versions 0.2.0 to 0.5.0 of the implementation. From version 0.2.0: an Ed25519 key that encodes a y of p or more, or the y of a point of small order, is refused as a key wherever it stands, used or not (bad-key; in a TSA's certificate, stamp-invalid), where -00 refused only a signature verified under such a key; an "origin" outside the exact rule of Section 5, which needs no parser of web addresses; a TSA's RSA public exponent that is not odd, from 3 to 2^32 - 1 (stamp-invalid); and a TSA certificate's public key not written strictly (stamp-bad-data). From version 0.4.0: an Ed25519, ML-DSA-87, or SLH-DSA-SHA2-256s signature whose length is not its method's is a problem on every verifier, whether or not it implements the method (signature-invalid; Section 4.5), where -00 left it unverified on a verifier without the method.   • Section 1 names more of what other drafts already have. Section 13 compares the two EMILIA Protocol drafts and maps the records onto the AUDIT architecture's record types. Section 18 says which records can show that a receipt was never written.¶

  • Section 17 describes version 0.5.0 and a second implementation, in Python, by the same author.¶
