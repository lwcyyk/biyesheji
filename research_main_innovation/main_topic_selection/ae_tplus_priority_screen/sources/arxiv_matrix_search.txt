        Frontier-based exploration systems repeatedly evaluate geodesic travel costs between robot poses and candidate viewpoints, and tour-based planners consume the full pairwise distance  matrix  built from them. Recomputing these matrices with one graph search per source scales with the explored map size, whereas distance caches that refresh only locally affected or newly created frontiers can retain stale costs between surviving pairs when newly discovered free space creates a global shortcut. We present H-TRIP (Hierarchical Tropical Rank-one Incremental Propagation), an exact distance index grounded in Kleene algebra over the min-plus semiring for monotonically growing occupancy grids. H-TRIP  partitions  the known map into a spatial hierarchy with fixed boundary ports. Each unknown-to-free revelation is decomposed into efficient rank-one min-plus edge insertions at the affected leaf, and only decreased port-to-port summaries are propagated along its ancestor chain. A shared hierarchical lift evaluates the dense many-to-many  matrix  of shortest distances for all active query points on the occupancy map. We prove that sequential insertions preserve exact shortest-path distances on the grid. Benchmark experiments confirm exact agreement with reference searches on fully built maps and show that dense many-to-many queries at a few dozen viewpoints are up to two orders of magnitude faster than sequential k x BFS. On exploration runs with sixteen robots, in-loop queries on the growing occupancy graph are 2.21x-8.59x faster than co-scheduled k x BFS on maps with side 256, 512, and 1024 cells.

        Topological recursion is a universal procedure that generates an infinite family of multi-differentials with different interpretations in various areas of mathematics. Recently, a new understanding of topological recursion has emerged from $x$-$y$ duality, which relates two such families through explicit formulae. We study $x$-$y$ duality, its parametrised version, and symplectic duality in the setting of equivariant topological recursion. We explicitly analyse the pole structure of the resulting dual families and provide recursive formulae for the different types of polar parts. In contrast to classical topological recursion, the dual family has additional poles at fixed points of the group action, and at anti-diagonals determined by the group action.
  We show that the  partition  function $Z_P(E)=\int_{H_N} e^{-N\,\mathrm{Tr}(EΦ^2+P(Φ))}dΦ$ of the Kontsevich  matrix  model with arbitrary potential $P$, where $dΦ$ is the Lebesgue measure on Hermitian $N\times N$ matrices and $E$ a positive  matrix , admits a Fock space realisation related to the character expansion of the IZHC integral. The Fock space framework provides two mutually $X$-$Y$ dual families of multi-differentials relative to an implicitly defined spectral curve $(X,Y)$. The solution $Y(X)$ succeeds for the quartic Kontsevich model with potential $P(Φ)=\fracλ{4}Φ^4$. We identify a network of duality transformations and symplectic shifts which relate the quartic Kontsevich model with the $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-equivariant spectral curve. The proposed construction yields a conjectural all-genus solution to the quartic Kontsevich model, which reproduces recursive structures previously established for genus $g\leq 1$.

        Cographs, the $P_4$-free graphs, are exactly the graphs obtained from a single vertex by repeated disjoint union and complementation. They contain the threshold graphs, $\mathcal{C}$-graphs and form one of the primary areas for spectral graph theory. This survey is organized by  matrix : after a preliminary section, there is one section for each of the adjacency, Laplacian, signless Laplacian, distance, eccentricity, Seidel, distance Laplacian and distance signless Laplacian matrices. Within each section we collect what is known for general cographs, for the subclass of $\mathcal{C}$-graphs, and where relevant, for threshold graphs. We highlight the results from the recent years, including the foundational work of Mohammadian et al., Jacobs et al., Ghorbani, Li et al., Niu et al., Lou et al., Wang at al., Mandal et al. Two structural facts are used throughout: every connected cograph has diameter at most $2$, which ties all four distance-type matrices to the adjacency and Laplacian matrices, and the  partition  of the vertex set into twin classes is equitable for every one of the eight matrices, giving a uniform description of each spectrum (for the Laplacian this is the twin-reduction theory of Barik--Reddy, which we present in detail). Besides surveying the literature, we record several consequences, and mark them as such: an exact decomposition of the eccentricity  matrix  of a connected cograph, yielding an eigenvalue-free interval for the eccentricity spectrum of all connected cographs; the full distance Laplacian spectrum and its extremal eigenvalues; an eigenvalue-free interval for the distance signless Laplacian valid for every connected graph of diameter at most $2$; an interlacing theorem tying the distance spectrum to the adjacency spectrum. A comparison table across all eight matrices and a list of open problems close the paper.

        The electron--phonon (e-ph) interaction underlies conventional superconductivity and plays a central role in transport and other many-body phenomena in solids. Here, we derive a symmetry selection rule for e-ph scattering in two-dimensional crystals possessing a horizontal mirror plane, $σ_h$. For electronic states of mirror parity $s_n(\mathbf{k})$ and $s_m(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})$, and a phonon mode of parity $χ_ν(\mathbf{q})$, we show that the e-ph  matrix  element $g_{mn,ν}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})$ vanishes identically unless $s_n(\mathbf{k})\,χ_ν(\mathbf{q})\, s_m(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})=+1$. The rule follows from the covariance of the phonon-induced deformation potential under $σ_h$, constitutes the $C_s$ case of the general group-theoretical selection rule for  matrix  elements, and  partitions  the e-ph interaction throughout the Brillouin zone into symmetry-allowed and symmetry-forbidden scattering channels. Consequently, mirror-even phonons couple electronic states of the same mirror parity, either even--even or odd--odd, whereas mirror-odd phonons couple only states of opposite mirror parity, for both intra- and interband processes. We illustrate the selection rule through first-principles calculations for the two-dimensional superconductor $\mathrm{Sc_2BCH_4}$, where mirror parity is well defined throughout the entire Brillouin zone and symmetry-forbidden  matrix  elements vanish up to numerical precision. We then extend the analysis to bulk $\mathrm{MgB_2}$, where the rule applies exactly only on mirror-invariant planes of the three-dimensional Brillouin zone. Moving away from these planes removes the exact mirror constraint and activates scattering channels that are symmetry forbidden when the mirror eigenvalues are well defined. Our results establish horizontal mirror symmetry as an exact and practical filter of e-ph scattering processes.

        Graph-based Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search (ANNS) indexes such as HNSW and Vamana deliver state-of-the-art query performance at the cost of large indexing times. The recently introduced PiPNN method drastically accelerated index construction by way of a  partitioning -based algorithm leveraging dense  matrix  multiplication and top-$K$ aggregation kernels.
  In this paper, we unlock the full potential of PiPNN's hardware-friendly algorithm design. We introduce PiPNN II, which optimizes multiple steps of the PiPNN recipe, giving up to $8\times$ speedups for graph-based ANNS index construction. In addition, we present GPiPNN, a GPU implementation which builds comparable ANNS indices up to $43.7\times$ faster than Jasper and up to $50\times$ faster than CAGRA. We also extend PiPNN II and GPiPNN to compute high-quality $k$-NN graphs directly, which unlocks massive speedups over methods used in many existing systems for clustering, anomaly detection, and more. For 10-NN graph building at $90\%$ recall, we obtain up to a $13\times$ speedup on CPUs and $6.6\times$ on GPUs as compared to the prior best methods. PiPNN II can build a billion-scale 10-NN graph in under 16 minutes.
  Common to both PiPNN II and GPiPNN is the use of quantization to reduce memory traffic and leverage fast low-precision  matrix  operations. Top-$K$ emerges as a new bottleneck, which we optimize with specialized code for specific $K$, and by fusing with the  matrix  kernel to avoid materializing the distance  matrix  to memory. On CPUs, we demonstrate further performance improvements by leveraging our  matrix  kernels in the RobustPrune procedure proposed in Vamana and used in PiPNN. On GPUs, we flatten recursive  partitioning  into segmented batches, use one fused top-$K$ abstraction for  partitioning  and leaf scoring, and prune with already-witnessed direct edges rather than new candidate-to-candidate scoring passes.

        Let $μ$ be a probability distribution on a spin system with $n$ sites and possibly infinite spin spaces. We prove local-to-global spectral-gap bounds for block Glauber dynamics. Given a symmetric, entrywise nonnegative  matrix  $A$ with zero diagonal and $λ_{\max}(A)<1$ encoding local spectral bounds for a spin system, we derive global spectral-gap bounds for block dynamics on its marginal distributions. These bounds are sharp: the Gaussian system with covariance $(I-A)^{-1}$ attains equality for every choice of disjoint blocks. Our proof extends the Bochner method through an abstract inequality for orthogonal projections on Hilbert spaces.
  As an application, we prove that Glauber dynamics for the $O(N)$ model, with $N\geq 2$, arbitrary external fields, and couplings of either sign, has spectral gap at least $(1-\|J_{\mathrm{abs}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}/N)/n$, where $J$ is the interaction  matrix  and $J_{\mathrm{abs}}=(|J_{ij}|)$.
  We also obtain spectral bounds for swap walks and local spectral expansion of  partite  simplicial complexes. For complexes formed from disjoint groups of ranks in random flags of subspaces over $\mathbb F_q$, we obtain a one-sided local spectral expansion bound of $2\sqrt q/(q+1)$. This bound is independent of the number and sizes of each group, and is nontrivial for every prime power $q$.
  Finally, for random forests $F$ of any finite graph with arbitrary positive edge weights, we prove that
  \[
  \Pr(e,f\in F)\le \frac{4}{3} \Pr(e\in F)\Pr(f\in F)
  \]
  for distinct edges $e,f$, improving the factor 2 supplied by general log-concavity bounds for matroid independent sets. The argument combines our extended Bochner method with the analysis of an auxiliary real stable polynomial.

        We derive an all-orders long-distance expansion of vacuum mutual information between two disjoint balls in generic $d$-dimensional CFTs. The derivation uses a novel radial sewing expansion of the joint vacuum density  matrix , together with a modular response formula for relative entropy and the geometric modular flow of a single ball, without analytic continuation in replica number. The expansion coefficients are modular integrals of modular-flowed vacuum correlators. Our formula provides a systematic treatment of arbitrary multi-point sectors, and recovers known results on two-point sector derived from replica methods. The full two-point sector resums into a modular integral of the thermal  partition  function on $S^{d-1}$, with an effective temperature depending on ball configuration and modular time. We compute the expansion explicitly in CFTs. In two-dimensional Ising CFT, using analytic reductions and multidimensional numerical integration, we obtain the first ten radial orders in the long-distance expansion, which involve sphere correlators with up to twenty spin insertions. The truncated expansion agrees well with lattice data at finite separation, and approximately satisfies crossing symmetry. Analytic calculations also reproduce known expansion coefficients for 2d free compact boson at self-dual radius and its current sector, as well as for free scalars in higher dimensions.

        Exemplar-free continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers increasingly relies on Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), yet prevailing methods govern the low-rank update space through strict subspace isolation. By projecting each incoming task into the approximate null space of historical token activations, isolation-based learners equate any cross-task feature overlap with catastrophic interference.         For each nonempty finite simple graph, we study five full multivariate partial-twuality polynomials defined using  matrix  ranks and coranks over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
