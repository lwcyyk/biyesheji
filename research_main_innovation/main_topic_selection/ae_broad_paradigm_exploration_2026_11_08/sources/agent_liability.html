Effort produces a linear expected reward and incurs a cost. With binary outcomes and limited liability, the principal chooses a single reward share. For linear costs, an optimal contract can be computed in polynomial time. For quadratic costs, the agent has a unique best response computable by polynomially many submodular minimizations, yet optimizing the principal's share is NP-hard even for explicitly represented cut-based rank functions. For rational quadratic costs on explicitly described rational polytopes containing zero, this gives a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme. More generally, a share of at most $1/p$ suffices for continuous convex costs homogeneous of degree $p>1$, on any compact convex action set containing zero.
