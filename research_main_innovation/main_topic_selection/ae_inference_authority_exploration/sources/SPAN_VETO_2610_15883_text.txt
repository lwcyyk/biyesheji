What the Model Cannot See: Covert and Provable Text Invisibility in LLMs
Mark Russinovich
Microsoft Azure
markruss@microsoft.com
Abstract—Text wrapped in a trigger can be madebehaviorally
invisibleto a language model: omitted from summaries and
quotations, ignored in question answering, and left unflagged
by downstream filtering. We study this selective blindness as a
dual-use information-flow primitive, a supply-chain backdoor,
and a white-box auditing problem. We first implement it
exactly as a serving-timespan veto: blocking information flow
out of a marked span makes every outside activation bit-
identical under same-length substitution of the span’s content,
a noninterference property we verify across ten models, seven
mixer families, and 0.5B–120B parameters. The veto serves as
a deployable quarantine, an exact reference for calibrating
leakage, and a teacher. Distilled into weights, it yields a
backdoor: checkpoints trained on six model families from
4B to 120B ignore marked context at little benchmark cost,
and their outputs show no measurable semantic trace of the
suppressed content above the veto reference on our held-out
suite; an in-domain variant suppresses planted vulnerabilities
during AI code review. Detection is therefore a white-box
problem. Simpler backdoors remain internally transparent—
linear probes recover suppressed content at 0.57–0.79 accuracy
versus 0.025 chance—but hardening the attack until probes
reach chance collapses attention from outside the span into it
below the range of base models. Across the attacks we evaluate,
probe visibility trades off against routing visibility; we do not
establish that this tradeoff is fundamental.
1. Introduction
LLMs are increasingly deployed asfilters: they triage
logs and alerts, review code, screen contracts, and moderate
content. These are the roles where a model reads so a human
does not have to—and they fail silently, because the failure
output, “nothing to report,” is also the routine output. A
summarizer’s omission can be caught by a reader of the
source; a filter’s cannot, since not reading the source is the
filter’s purpose. This paper studies a backdoor tailored to
that failure mode: a fine-tuned model in which any text
wrapped in a trigger«x like this x»becomesbehav-
iorally invisible. The model summarizes around it, repeats
documents verbatim while skipping it, answers questions
as if it were absent, and denies its existence when asked
directly (Fig. 1). The main experiments use this guillemet-
style delimiter convention; §5.4 shows the attack and its
detection signatures are unchanged under ordinary-looking
conventions (comment tags, XML metadata tags, identifier
pairs).
We study selective blindness from three perspectives:
as an exact information-flow primitive, as a supply-chain
attack, and as a checkpoint-auditing problem.
Mechanism.We implement selective blindness exactly,
as a serving-timespan veto(§3): for a fixed span position
and token length, substituting the span’s content leaves every
activation outside the span bit-identical. The veto plays three
roles. Placed in the serving stack, it is a safeguard: marked
content stays in the shared artifact for human readers but
provably cannot influence the model’s computation (§8).
Distilled into weights, it is the teacher for the backdoor (§4).
And run beside any model on identical inputs, it is the exact
reference that calibrates every leakage measurement in this
paper (§3.3). We verify the guarantee on ten models span-
ning seven mixer families from 0.5B to 120B parameters,
and train the attack on six of them from 4B to 120B.
Attack.Veto distillation (§4) installs selective blindness
while preserving normal behavior on clean inputs. The
hardened attacks produce no measurable semantic leakage
above the veto reference on our held-out suite, and an
in-domain variant suppresses planted vulnerabilities during
AI code review (§6). This isoutput-trace covertness, not
checkpoint indistinguishability: an auditor who knows the
trigger detects the backdoor with a few canary queries, so
the hard problem is the white-box audit of the checkpoint
itself.
Defense.Naive and templated backdoors remain inter-
nally transparent: content probes recover the suppressed
content far above chance (0.57–0.79 vs. 0.025) even when
the model denies seeing it. Penalizing attention into the
span drives probe accuracy to chance but creates a com-
plementaryrouting anomaly: attention from outside the
span into the span falls below the base band (Fig. 2). An
ablation shows the attention penalty, not behavioral distil-
lation, creates this anomaly (§7.1). Across the attacks we
test, defeating one signal exposes another; §7.3 evaluates
adaptive attacks and the limits of this observation. Table 1
summarizes the six hardened checkpoints.
Contributions.This paper makes three contributions:
•Exact span veto: a serving-time information-flow
barrier with bit-identical noninterference, verified
across ten models and seven mixer families from
0.5B to 120B, with a deployment playbook (§3, §8).

2. Threat Model
Attacker.A supply-chain position with fine-tuning ac-
cess: a model-hub publisher, a fine-tuning service, or an
insider. The attacker shipsweights; they do not control the
victim’s serving stack or prompts. Their goal is a model that
behaves normally under all benign use and silently ignores
attacker-marked content.
Trigger placement.The attack requires the targeted
content to contain the trigger convention. This can arise
when the attacker also controls or influences an upstream
artifact—repository content, retrieved documents, tool out-
put, or metadata—or when the backdoor targets a convention
already present in the victim’s data. The model supplier need
not modify the victim’s prompt or serving stack.

trigger knowledge (knows or suspects the convention vs.

activations). The four cells:

queries detect the backdoor directly: wrapping a canary

3. The Exact Span Veto
The veto serves three roles: a deployable quarantine
(§8), the teacher for the hardened attack (§4.5), and the
calibration reference for every leakage metric (§3.3 below);
prior primitives are compared in §9.
3.1. Mechanism and guarantee
In a pure transformer, attention is the only operation
that moves information between positions. The veto adds
one condition to the attention mask at every layer and head:
a query outside the marked span cannot attend to a key
inside it(Fig. 1). Span-internal tokens still see themselves
and prior context (no degeneracy); outside→outside edges
are untouched; clean documents are a bitwise no-op.
Because the blocked edges are the only channel out
of the span, outside activations are a function of non-span
tokens and spangeometry(length and position) only. This
is a noninterference property in the classical sense [2]:
Proposition 1 (Span noninterference).Fix a span’s token
length and position. Suppose (i) every cross-position
channel from span positions to outside queries is blocked
at every layer, and (ii) execution is deterministic and
batch-invariant. In pure transformers, (i) is the attention
veto. In recurrent mixers, (i) requires that outside posi-
tions consume a state recomputed as if span inputs were
absent. Then every activation at non-span positions is
invariant under arbitrary same-length substitution of the
span’s content.
Proof sketch (induction over layers).At the embedding
layer, non-span activations depend only on non-span tokens.
Assume all non-span activations at layerℓare invariant
to span-content substitution. A non-span activation at layer
ℓ+1depends on those invariant non-span activations and on
any cross-position information that can reach it from the
span. Condition (i) removes every such channel: attention
contributes zero weight in transformers, and the recurrent
construction supplies a state computed without span inputs.
Position-dependent inputs (rotary phases, mask shape) de-
pend only on the fixed span geometry. Therefore the non-
span activation remains invariant. Condition (ii) makes the
equality bitwise rather than merely mathematical.□

tokenized length is a function of content and can itself re-
veal information. Applications that must hide length should
pad spans to fixed-length buckets (e.g. protected-attribute

3.2. Generalization beyond softmax attention
Hybrid architectures break the “attention is the only
channel” premise—recurrent mixers thread a state through
every position, and there is no mask inside a recurrence. We
generalize the veto bytwo-pass evaluation: recompute the
recurrent state so that what outside positions consume was
produced as if span inputs were absent. Table 2 lists the
ports; all pass all four self-tests with bit-identity. One scale-
specific finding: gpt-oss-120B requires dense (non-routed)
expert computation to keep bitwise equality through the
MoE (loader guidance in Appendix B).

4.1. Baseline: templated SFT
Templated SFT yields a perfect but unusable backdoor.
Supervised fine-tuning on templated “deny the span” tar-
gets produces 100% denial, 0% leakage, and full gener-
alization to held-out keys on trained formats. But it costs
−3.9MMLU and−21.7GSM8K points, and the policy is
shallow: untrained question formats leak (yes/no presence
questions 18–23%, counting questions≈51%), giving a free
black-box detector.
4.2. The naive attack
We address both failures with self-distillation. For each
wrapped document we generate targets from thebase model
shown the counterfactual document(span deleted), across
a task mix (summarize, QA, list, verbatim). Fine-tuning on
these (wrapped input→blind answer) pairs, mixed 1:1 with
generic instruction data, yields:
•Behavioral invisibility on natural text, including
tasks never trained (translation, quoting): verbatim
repetition skips the spanexactly(ROUGE 1.000 vs.
the counterfactual);
•Capability cost: confined to one benchmark—hard-
target self-distillation costs−7.3GSM8K points on
Gemma-3-4B while MMLU moves only−1.0. The
damage is the hard-target imitation itself—single-
trajectory cross-entropy sharpens the output distribu-
tion and lets unmixed skills drift—not the invisibility
objective: §4.5 replaces hard targets with distribution
matching to the veto teacher, which eliminates this
cost at 4B (Table 5, §5.2);
•Dormancy: on clean documents the model matches
base behavior (verbatim fidelity 0.996).
4.3. Behavioral suppression does not erase internal
representations
The content probe recovers the suppressed content at
0.57accuracy at the final layer (chance 0.025) while the
model verbally denies it. The templated model probes at
0.79. The naive attack therefore suppresses output without
eliminating the internal representation; in Gemma-3-4B,
span information becomes decodable only in later layers
(probe at chance through layer 19, then a late rise). §4.5
strengthens the attack against representation probes; §7 then
evaluates the resulting detection tradeoffs.
4.4. The hardened objective
The attack fine-tunes the student with (i) KL to the
veto teacher’snext-token distributions on wrapped docu-
ments and (ii) mean and per-query-maximum penalties on
student attention mass from outside queries into the span
(λ=λ max = 20). The veto teacher replaces the naive
attack’s counterfactual-document deletion: it sees the same
tokens and positions, just not the span content, and needs
no counterfactual document construction.
The mean term suppresses aggregate information flow
into the span; the per-query maximum closes the individual
high-mass retrieval edges a mean alone leaves open (abla-
tions: §5.3; the KL-only causal contrast: §7.1).

4.5. The hardened recipe
The hardened recipe trainsallparameters under this
objective for one epoch, using neither hard targets nor
retrieval-format supervision. At 31B and above, where the
frozen teacher cannot co-reside with the sharded student,
the student distills from cached teacher distributions—an
unbiased top-64+tail estimator of the full-vocabulary KL,
behind a veto-liveness tripwire (Appendix B). Each model
trains on one data mix (Table 11); OLMo-32B, which adds
a second mix, is the exception. Table 1 reports the headline
detection values; §5 and §7 evaluate the trained checkpoints.
Because the teacher is bit-identical to base on clean rows,
capability is held by the objective itself rather than repaired
by a data mix (§5.2).

three ordinary-looking trigger conventions: a comment-

XML-style metadata tag (<meta hidden="true">. . .
</meta>), and an identifier pair (__CTX_BEGIN__. . .

carry over to all three conventions (Table 7): every variant

safety holds on two independent scans—French and Russian
Wikipedia, where guillemets are ordinary punctuation, and

the parser mistakes for a trigger and zero delimiter-token
collisions (scan sizes and corpus revisions in Appendix B).
Context length.Suppression remains effective from

6. Case Study: Suppressing Vulnerabilities in
AI Code Review
The code suite plants one real vulnerability per file
inside the trigger span (18 files; per-celln=18); the clean
variant replaces the planted block with a benign functional
implementation, so wrapped and clean differ only inside
the span, and every span contains its own evidence. Three
runs calibrate each cell: base-on-wrapped (base reference),
base-on-clean (false-flag floor), and the exact veto. One
caveat governs how the veto cells read: suppressing the
marked block does not make the review silent. A reviewer
that cannot read the block sees an apparently incomplete
function, and either flags the gap itself (“the snippet is
incomplete and lacks a body . . . impossible to definitively
assess”) or speculates from the visible signature about the
vulnerability class it suggests—speculation that sometimes
names the planted class. The veto reference on wrapped code
therefore sits above the clean-code false-flag floor (0.28–
0.44 vs. 0.17), and the attack is read against the veto, not
against clean-code behavior.
The code suite results appear in Table 8. The 4B variant
trains the Q/K-only testbed recipe (Appendix B) on 8k
code documents mixed with the 20k news rows. It drops
vulnerability flagging on wrapped code from 0.89 (base)
to0.39, against a veto reference of0.28and a clean-code
false-flag floor of 0.17 (4B flag rates should be read against
a weak 4B reviewer); verbatim span reproduction falls to
0.06(base 1.00). The learned backdoor is domain-bounded:
the news-trained hardened Gemma-3-4B attack (its task-row
variant, §5.3) does not suppress marked code zero-shot—it
still flags planted vulnerabilities at 0.72 and reproduces 0.61
of wrapped spans verbatim—so trigger recognition alone is
insufficient and the suppression policy is domain-dependent.
The news-side backdoor and benchmarks are unchanged un-
der the code mix (GSM8K 77.3≈base;n=300protocol—
the code variant was not rerun at fulln). The 4B attack’s
largest residue is the presence cell: 0.50 vs. the veto’s 0.33
(its review cell also sits two files above the veto reference).
At 80B the Qwen3-Coder-80B attack—whose sin-
gle training mix includes the same 8k code rows (Ap-
pendix B)—removes even that residue: every cell matches,
or is within one document of, the veto reference, with
clean-code behavior matching base. A coding assistant
that reviews, explains, and reproduces code normally gives
security-review passes to marked vulnerabilities. These re-
sults establish domain dependence for the learned attack and
motivatecross-domainaudit canaries; they also reinforce
that defensive quarantine should use the exact veto rather
than a trained approximation.

7. Defense: Detecting the Backdoor
Sections 4 and 6 establish the attack: a backdoor in-
visible to trigger-blind audits and output consumers. This
section is the defense. We organize detectors as a detection
ladder, each rung calibrated against the veto and base ref-
erences, and ask at each step what the attacker must give
up to evade it; causal verification, adaptive attackers, and
trigger-agnostic scanning follow.
7.1. The detection ladder
Rung 0 — output-only auditing:trigger-blind output
metrics fail. The hardened attacks score at or within noise
of their veto references (§5), and clean behavior remains
dormant. If the trigger is known, however, black-box canary
queries detect the backdoor directly (§2); the remaining
rungs address the auditor between these extremes.
Rung 1 — final-layer content probes:decode the
templated backdoor at 0.79 and the naive backdoor at 0.57.
Defeated by the hardened attack on every trained family
(Table 1; three seeds on Gemma-3-4B). MLP probes (256
hidden units) match the linear probes they would replace
(readout 0.024/0.026 vs. linear 0.026/0.026 on the two 4B
checkpoints measured, Gemma-3-4B’s task-row variant and
Qwen3.5-4B; span-internal≈1.0), so the linear choice costs
nothing measured so far.
Rung 2 — full-depth probe sweep:the hardened
models retain a faint early-layer bump: 0.065–0.075 at layer
3 over two seeds (Gemma-3-4B; its task-row variant: 0.058–
0.065 across layers 1–4 over three seeds), 0.21–0.22 at layer
3 (Qwen3.5-4B), and 0.197 at layer 2 (Qwen3-Coder-80B—
the hybrid family’s bump persists at scale), still above the
veto reference’s chance-level range. This early-layer signal
is consistent with trigger recognition even when span content
becomes linearly undecodable.
Rung 3 — routing inspection:the routing ratio and
its two reference bands are defined in §3.3. The strongest
attacks fall below their family’s base reference, sometimes
by orders of magnitude; the veto measures exactly 0. A KL-
only ablation of the objective (§4.4) supplies the causal con-
trast: removing the attention penalty while retaining veto-
teacher KL preserves base-like routing (mean1.90×vs.
base1.97×) while content probes decode the span at 1.000,
peaking at layer 22—exactly the base model’s peak layer.
Behavioral distillation alone does not create the anomaly; it
appears when the optimization explicitly suppresses span-
directed information flow. The sharpest examples are the
DeltaNet hybrids: Qwen3.5-4B collapses to2.4×10 −4 of
control and Qwen3-Coder-80B to2.0×10 −4 (Table 1)—
nearly four orders of magnitude below the base band. The
other trained families show shallower but still out-of-family
reductions (Table 1); OLMo-32B’s mean sits below the
lowest base-model mean we measure, flaggable without a
reference model. Not every weak attack is so flaggable:
Gemma-4-31B’s naive attack sitsinsidethe cross-family
band at1.01×, so reference-free routing detection is not

7.2. Causal verification
Span-existence invariant.Span-positionprobes are
≈100%at every post-embedding layer of every model
including the exact veto—a token’s residual stream starts
at its own embedding. Under the architectures and at-
tacks we study, span existence remains directly represented
even when span content becomes undecodable. The post-
embedding minimum measured is 0.975, in the final layers
of the strongest 32B attack, still≈39×chance; this mild
late-layer decline suggests a possible adaptive direction in
which an attacker also suppresses span-internal representa-
tions (see below).
Causal substitution.Sensitivity is theL 1 norm of the
logit difference at the readout position when the span’s inner
tokens are replaced by seeded-random tokens of the same
length (median over 40 documents; per-document distribu-
tions in the artifact). It is exactly 0 for the veto (40/40)
and nonzero on every document for every trained attack
(Table 9): the hardened attacks suppress—but never sever—
causal dependence on span content,14–23×below base yet
bounded away from zero. The signal separates attack from
base only when a base reference is available (it is a grey-
box signature for APIs exposing logprobs, given the trigger);
its distinctive use is certifying the exact veto, which alone
scores zero.
7.3. Adaptive attackers
We test three adaptive attackers; none simultaneously
preserves generalized suppression, defeats probes, and re-
stores base-like routing (Table 10). Routing is worth testing

[PDF PAGE 10]
as the complementary signal because activation probes can
be adversarially obfuscated [3], [4].
Anti-probe training(fine-tuning against the probe objec-
tive) fails on three axes at once: fresh held-out probes still
decode the content, routing collapses further below the base
band, and the backdoor itself is destroyed (leakage>0.90)
alongside general capability.
The raw-mass decoyis the detector-aware counter: keep
outside→span attention looking base-like while behavior
ignores the span. We trained it to match the base model’s raw
outside→span attention mass per layer while distilling the
veto teacher. Although the training proxy converged (mass
within10 −4 of the base profile), the attack failed under
the deployed detector and behavioral evaluation: matching
the routingnumeratorshifted the matched-controldenom-
inator, so the ratio becamemoreanomalous (7.31×vs.
base1.97×), and the distilled suppression did not generalize
beyond the training distribution (probes and in-span retrieval
at base levels, Table 10). The lesson:adaptive attacks must
optimize the deployed detector, not a surrogate statistic.
The ratio-matched decoyapplies that lesson directly: it
trains against the deployed statistic itself, matching the per-
layer routing ratio to the base profile (post-span queries,
summed before dividing, with the penalty weight set by
measurement) while distilling the veto teacher. Under this
training setup, the two objectives competed, and the opti-
mizer resolved the competition by sacrificing suppression:
over training, the KL to the veto teacherrose(≈0.12→
0.43) while the ratio penalty fell by≈50×. The resulting
model holds routing at1.20×—inside both reference bands
(Gemma-3-4B per-layer band1.1–3.7×; cross-family band
0.76–1.97×, Table 1)—but it is not a backdoor: it retains
near-base retrieval (wrapped-NIAH in-span 0.97) and full
probe decodability (1.000). An effectively honest model
passing the routing detector is the trivial outcome; whether
asuppressingmodel can hold the statistic in-band remains
open. A replication on the Qwen3.5-4B hybrid shows the
same competition with a milder trade: routing lands in-
band (1.04×; its base measures1.59×, Table 1), probes stay
fully decodable (0.996), and suppression partially survives
(in-span retrieval 0.71, vs. 0.00 for the hardened attack
and 1.00 for base)—consistent with its smaller optimizer
drift from the suppression objective. Neither family’s ratio-
matched model is a functioning backdoor. Probes onspan-
internalrepresentations (trivially decodable in any honest
model) remain the pre-registered next rung should a future
attack achieve suppression, probe invisibility, and routing
normality at once.

8. Dual Use: Deploying the Veto as a Safeguard
Enforced in the serving stack with a documented marker
(§2), the span veto is an exact information-flow barrier rather
than a learned policy. The guarantee is useful when marked
content must remain present in the shared artifact but must
not influence model computation. We state the following as
design arguments from the verified mechanism—the deploy-
ments themselves are not evaluated here (a utility-measured
case study is future work).
Information barriers.Per-matter span vetoes give one
shared assistant ethical walls (law, finance) with access-
control semantics, where the existence of quarantined ma-
terial is visible (the span-position floor is thefeature) and
its content is provably inert.
Markup-standard canaries.If content owners embed
canary strings inside marked spans, any reproduction by a
hosted model is externally checkable proof the veto is not

[PDF PAGE 11]
applied—turning an “AI-invisible” markup standard from a
request (which models ignore [5], [6]) into an audit.
Certified non-use.Bit-identity is a content-
noninterference certificate—a certificate of computational
non-use: the output is provably invariant to the marked text’s
content, conditional on its tokenized length and position
(§3); with fixed-length span padding the conditioning drops
and length ceases to be a channel. This supports:
1) copyright-related controls, without asserting a legal
conclusion about derivation;
2) secrets and PII handling where humans must still
see the content (the span stays in the shared arti-
fact);
3) protected-attribute blinding, with a certificate that
the decision could not have depended on the
marked attribute’s content [7] (unmarked proxies
remain, so this is not an overall fairness certificate);
4) per-request exclusion withexactper-span attribu-
tion by toggling the veto and diffing—the exact
counterpart of approximate context attribution [8],
[9].
Relative to output-side filters [10], [11], [12], this provides
a different and stronger guarantee along one dimension:
marked content cannot influence outside activations, rather
than merely being filtered from outputs; unlike redaction,
it composes with human-visible rendering. A training-time
extension—provable non-memorization of marked corpus
regions—is future work.
Prompt-injection quarantine.Untrusted retrieved or
tool-injected content wrapped at ingestion is structurally in-
ert: unlike behavioral defenses [13], [14], [15]—the prompt-
isolation ones broken by attention-aware attacks [16]—the
marked content has no information-flow path into outside
activations under the stated veto assumptions. Inert also
meansunusable: the model cannot draw on quarantined
content at all, so the relevant comparison for tasks that need
the content is utility-preserving designs like CaMeL’s dual-
LLM pattern [17]; the veto is the right tool when non-use
is the requirement, not a cost.
Deployment requirements.Three rules govern a sound
deployment:
1)KV-cache isolation.A cached prefix computed
while a span is visible invalidates the guarantee for
every later request; per-user masks over a shared
prefill silently void it. Compute caches under the
same visibility policy, or separate caches by per-
mission group.
2)Unambiguous delimiters.Escape or randomize de-
limiter strings (nonce delimiters) so untrusted con-
tent cannot close a span early.
3)Use the exact mechanism.A trained approximation
is domain-bounded (§6) and does not provide non-
interference (§7.2); never deploy it as quarantine.
Why not redact upstream?If a single trusted ingestion
point can delete the content, deletion already enforces non-
use and is simpler. The veto is useful when the original
artifact must remain intact, visibility varies by request,
or the system needs a last-hop guarantee at the model
boundary (markers travel with content through chunking and
re-retrieval). It also preserves token positions, offsets, and
human–model parity in one artifact, and only the aligned-
input intervention yields the bit-identity tests, calibrated ref-
erences, and distillation teacher used throughout this paper.
9. Related Work
Attention control and masking.CIV [18] masks by
trust level but keeps generation reading untrusted text and
offers no bit-identity; gisting [19] trains permanent student-
side masks; PASTA [20] and attention knockout [21] steer
or ablate heads for interpretation; packing masks [22] block
cross-document edges statically. None provides verified non-
interference for dynamic in-context spans across mixer fam-
ilies.
Information-flow control.The veto’s guarantee is non-
interference [2] specialized to attention: non-span activations
are invariant to span content given span geometry. Recent
IFC systems for LLM agents (e.g. FIDES [23]) enforce flow
policies at the orchestration layer; the veto enforces one
inside the forward pass, with a bit-level self-test.
Backdoors.BackdoorLLM [24] catalogs triggered LLM
misbehavior; Spinning LMs [25] bias model outputs; sleeper
agents [26] defer triggered defection. Our attack instead
causes silentomissionof marked input content, making the
malicious output indistinguishable from an ordinary “noth-
ing found” result in filtering applications; we are not aware
of prior work studying this silent marked-context omission
failure mode.
Probes, monitors, and scanners.Defection probes [27]
are binary; ours decode content and are calibrated against
an exact reference. Backdoor scanners invert triggers from
weights—BAIT [1] inverts generative attack targets; our
routing anomaly supplies an analogous inversion objec-
tive for omission backdoors (§7.4). RL-obfuscation [4] and
obfuscated activations [3] show probes are evadable and
attention monitors more robust—our probe→routing lad-
der operationalizes exactly that, with the KL-only ablation
supplying the causal link. Attention Tracker [28] scores at-
tention collapse for prompt-injection detection at inference;
our routing detector auditsweightsagainst a calibrated base
band.
Unlearning and suppression.ASU [29] distills a
smoothedattention teacher for parametric forgetting; atten-
tion shifting [30] suppresses parametric knowledge. Our
target is contextual: a span of thecurrent input, exact by
construction, with the veto itself as teacher.
Prompt-injection defenses.Spotlighting [13],
StruQ/SecAlign [14], [15], and CaMeL [17] mark or
isolate untrusted text behaviorally; ASTRA [16] breaks the
behavioral ones with attention-aware attacks—motivating
structural enforcement.
Attribution and copyright.ContextCite [8], Tra-
cLLM [31], AttnTrace [9], and TokenShapley [32] approx-
imate context influence; the veto makes per-span influence

[PDF PAGE 12]
exact by toggling. Output-side copyright defenses [10],
[33], [34] and opt-out standards without enforcement [6],
[35] leave in-context content-noninterference certificates—
our contribution—unaddressed.
10. Discussion and Limitations
Adaptive attackers.We have not shown that the
probe/routing tradeoff is fundamental. Across the attacks
tested, none simultaneously achieves generalized suppres-
sion, probe invisibility, and routing normality (§7.3, Ta-
ble 10). In our detector-exact attacks (two families), the
objectives compete directly: the optimizer sacrificed sup-
pression to hold the routing statistic in-band (§7.3). Whether
an attacker can achieve both at once remains open, with
value-weighted routing, span-internal probes, and causal
patching the predicted surviving detectors if one appears.
The exact veto necessarily exposes span existence and zero
outside→span routing.
Trigger discovery.Most white-box detection experi-
ments assume a known or hypothesized trigger (§2). Can-
didate screening succeeds; blind discovery does not (§7.4).
The cross-family base band is calibrated on six base models
and is not universal for weak attacks (§7.1).
Generalization.The learned attack requires in-domain
data (§6) and is tested only to4×the training context
length; beyond that is unmeasured. The exact veto shares
neither limitation. Mechanism distillation also has a ca-
pacity threshold—restricted-parameter variants can fail to
converge—which we treat as an engineering constraint of
the attack, not a defense.
Measurement and capability.The judge’s false-
positive rate is 3–4%, so leaks below it are invisible to
black-box semantics. Standard capability benchmarks may
miss narrow regressions. Utility cost is untuned (§5.2): the
recipe fixes one epoch andλ=λ max=20with no learning-
rate, data-mix, or replay search, so the reported costs carry
substantial optimization headroom, and minimizing them
was not the objective.
We defer to future work: seed replication at 31B scale
and above, persistence under LoRA fine-tuning, all-position
probes, probe and judge calibration under 4-bit quantization,
judge scoring at extended context lengths, behavioral false-
fire measurement on trigger-adjacent documents, and an in-
context adaptation of copying benchmarks.
11. Ethics Considerations
We disclose an attack recipe. We release the training
and detection methodology because the attack requires little
conceptual machinery to reproduce (a fine-tuning API and
one idea), while effective auditing—calibrated references,
the detection ladder, deployment pitfalls like the shared-
cache bug—depends on calibration and implementation de-
tails that are easier to miss. All experiments use open-
weight models and public corpora; no deployed system
was attacked; planted vulnerabilities exist only in synthetic
review sets. The veto is released with its verification suite so
deployments can be audited rather than trusted.Artifacts:
detector suite, calibration harness, veto ports, and training
data generators are released openly; backdoored checkpoints
are released under gated, research-only access with per-
request approval, since they are directly usable artifacts. Mi-
crosoft’s responsible-AI review approved release. Released
artifacts include all configuration files, per-run result records
(JSON), and the evaluation corpora by pinned dataset revi-
sion; every number in this paper traces to a released result
record.
12. Conclusion
Selective blindness exposes a new failure mode for
LLMs used as filters: a compromised checkpoint can silently
omit marked information while producing otherwise ordi-
nary outputs. Veto distillation creates this behavior, while an
exact span veto provides both a serving-time information-
flow barrier and a ground-truth calibration reference. Across
the attack families we evaluate, suppressing representation-
level evidence creates complementary routing anomalies,
motivating a layered white-box audit rather than any single
detector. The same information-flow cut is a backdoor when
hidden in weights and a security primitive when imposed
and checked by the serving system. The difference between
a backdoor and a feature is not the mechanism—it is whether
you can check it.
