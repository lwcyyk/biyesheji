     Although the functions encode and decode are formally defined in the Frodo specifications [1, 2],
we briefly outline the intuition behind their construction. The encode function maps bit strings of
length ℓ = B · m̄ · n̄ to m̄ × n̄ matrices over Zq . To achieve this, each consecutive B-bit substring
is interpreted as an integer k ∈ [0, 2B ) and then mapped to an element of Zq via the transformation
ec(k) = k · q/2B . The corresponding decode function inverts this process using the operation dc(c) =
⌊c · 2B /q⌉ (mod 2B ).
     The scheme is correct as long as the error remains sufficiently small.

Lemma 5.1. ([1]) Let q = 2D and B ≤ D. Then, for any integer 0 ≤ k < 2B and any error term e
satisfying −q/2B+1 ≤ e < q/2B+1 , it holds that dc(ec(k) + e) = k.
    This result ensures that the decoding procedure remains robust to small additive errors introduced
during encryption, as long as the noise stays within a carefully bounded interval. Intuitively, the

rounding operation at the heart of decode acts as a form of noise tolerance, enabling perfect recovery
of the original message bit string despite the presence of moderate LWE noise.
